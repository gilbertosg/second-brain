# L1 — Biomarker Digest: Kidney

**What this group shows:** how well the kidneys filter waste (creatinine, BUN, eGFR), whether protein leaks into urine (random urine albumin), and sodium, the electrolyte the kidneys help regulate. All five are in range at both draws. Function files calcium, chloride, potassium, and carbon dioxide under Electrolytes ([[L1-Health/biomarker-digest-electrolytes]]).

**How to read this page:** each entry paraphrases Function Health's explainer text from the raw file in its **Source** line. Results are as of Oct 2, 2026; [[L1-Health/function-health-registry]] stays the source of truth for values. All five entries are Tier 2. Vault context: [[L1-Health/biomarker-reference]] (organ health table) lists creatinine and BUN as healthy.

## Albumin, Random Urine without Creatinine — in range (no lab range)

**Measures:** albumin protein in a random urine sample (microalbumin), reported without a urine creatinine to normalize it.

**Why it matters:** albumin normally stays in the blood. Leakage into urine is one of the earliest signs of kidney stress, often from high blood pressure or type 2 diabetes, and can appear before creatinine or BUN change. Function states a random urine albumin without creatinine has limited use without a follow-up albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR) and should not be read alone.

**Your result:** < 0.2 mg/dL (May 19) → 0.4 mg/dL (Oct 2). The lab supplies no reference range, so Function's page marks it in range and advises discussing it with a clinician. Function also lists this marker under Urine.

## Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN) — in range

**Measures:** urea, the liver's waste product from protein breakdown, which the kidneys filter out.

**Why it matters:** high BUN can mean the kidneys filter poorly, but also dehydration or heavy protein intake; the liver makes urea, so liver function and protein intake move it. Clinicians compare it with creatinine. A high BUN without a high creatinine suggests excess dietary protein, often from supplemental amino acids.

**High / low:** high BUN reflects poor kidney filtration, dehydration, high protein intake, or protein breakdown from illness or internal bleeding. Low BUN can mean liver dysfunction, malnutrition, a very low-protein diet, or overhydration. Function lists 6–24 mg/dL as optimal. Hydration and recent protein intake move it.

**Your result:** 12 mg/dL (May 19) → 15 mg/dL (Oct 2), in range. Quest and Function optimal 7–25.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/blood-urea-nitrogen-bun.md)

## Creatinine — in range

**Measures:** a waste product of muscle metabolism, made at a steady rate and cleared by the kidneys. Function notes it is not the supplement creatine.

**Why it matters:** it is a main marker of kidney function, and because it comes from muscle, people with more muscle run higher baselines. It matters most with diabetes, high blood pressure, or medications that can harm the kidneys, and clinicians read it with BUN. Function adds that a value in the optimal range does not rule out early kidney damage, since creatinine responds late.

**High / low:** high creatinine can mean poor kidney filtration. Low creatinine can mean low muscle mass, malnutrition, fluid overload, pregnancy, or liver disease. Function's optimal range is 0.57–1.00 mg/dL. Muscle mass, hard exercise, cooked meat, and hydration move it.

**Your result:** 1.09 mg/dL (May 19) → 0.97 mg/dL (Oct 2), in Quest range (0.60–1.26); above Function's optimal band in May and inside it in Oct.

## Creatinine-Based Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) — in range

**Measures:** an estimate of how much blood the kidney filters per minute, calculated from creatinine plus age, sex, and other factors.

**Why it matters:** it is the standard kidney-function figure and stages chronic kidney disease. Creatinine-based eGFR shifts with muscle mass and diet, and a cystatin C version can be more accurate in some people. A value below 60 for three months or more signals chronic kidney disease, and below 15 signals severe impairment. Function reads it with creatinine, the urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and cystatin C.

**High / low:** Function's optimal range is 90–120 mL/min/1.73m². It declines naturally with age, and adults over 70 can be normal between 60 and 89. Hydration, recent exercise, protein intake, weight, medications, and illness move it.

**Your result:** 92 mL/min/1.73m² (May 19) → 106 mL/min/1.73m² (Oct 2), in range. Quest ≥ 60; Function optimal 90–120.

## Sodium — in range

**Measures:** the main electrolyte in blood, taken in as table salt and processed food.

**Why it matters:** sodium regulates fluid balance, nerve signaling, and muscle contraction, including the heart. Recent hydration can shift the reading temporarily. The kidneys, adrenal glands, and ADH signaling keep it tight.

**High / low:** too much sodium can raise blood pressure, strain the kidneys, worsen fatty liver outcomes, and cause swelling. Too little (hyponatremia) can cause headaches, nausea, fatigue, confusion, and cramps, and in severe cases seizures or coma. Function's optimal range follows the lab's, 135–146 mmol/L. Hydration, diet, diuretics, and kidney or adrenal disorders move it.

**Your result:** 141 mmol/L (May 19) → 139 mmol/L (Oct 2), in range. Quest and Function optimal 135–146.
