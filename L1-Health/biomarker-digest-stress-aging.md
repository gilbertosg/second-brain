## Cortisol — in range

**Measures:** the adrenal stress hormone, in a blood sample.

**Why it matters:** cortisol shapes the stress response, energy use, inflammation control, memory formation, and immune and nervous system balance. It peaks in the early morning to help waking, falls at night, and works with insulin on blood sugar and with thyroid hormones. Function reads it with DHEA-S, ACTH, and glucose to see patterns of stress, fatigue, inflammation, or metabolic health. A blood draw shows overall output; saliva tests show the daily rhythm better.

**High / low:** high cortisol from long-term stress can come with Cushing's syndrome, anxiety, depression, or poor sleep and adds belly weight and blood sugar trouble. Low cortisol may signal adrenal insufficiency or Addison's disease (extreme tiredness, salt cravings, low blood pressure). Function states its optimal range follows the lab values when the draw happens between 7:00 and 9:00 a.m. Values peak 30–45 minutes after waking and shift with stress, sleep, illness, activity, diet, and even a stressful drive to the lab.

**Your result:** 8.0 mcg/dL (May 19) → 14.7 mcg/dL (Oct 2), in range. Quest and Function optimal 4–22. Function's export records no draw time, so the comparison against its 7:00–9:00 a.m. basis stays open. The vault's earlier "low cortisol" reading came from Jan 2026 (7.6, Spren).

## DHEA Sulfate — in range at Quest (below Function's age benchmark)

**Measures:** an adrenal androgen that serves as a building block for testosterone and DHT.

**Why it matters:** DHEA-S influences energy, mood, immunity, metabolism, and blood pressure, peaks in early adulthood, and declines with age, so it marks adrenal and metabolic health. It tracks with insulin sensitivity and body composition. Function reads it with testosterone, cortisol, and androstenedione to judge adrenal function and stress response.

**High / low:** high DHEA-S can mean overactive adrenals or congenital adrenal hyperplasia; low DHEA-S can mean adrenal insufficiency, aging, or chronic illness. Function says optimal levels run higher than the lab range: 500–640 mcg/dL for younger men, 400–500 for middle-aged men, 300–400 for older men, and that a result inside the lab range can still be suboptimal when fatigue, low libido, or low mood are present. Stress, poor sleep, glucocorticoids, and blood sugar lower it; intense exercise raises it temporarily.

**Your result:** 234 mcg/dL (May 19) → 212 mcg/dL (Oct 2), in range. Quest 93–415. Against Function's 500–640 benchmark for younger men the Oct value is well under. The vault's Spren band (260–520) points the same direction.

## Insulin-like Growth Factor (IGF-1) — in range (May only)

**Measures:** the liver-made protein that carries growth hormone's instructions to cells; a steady proxy for long-term growth hormone activity.

**Why it matters:** IGF-1 builds bone, muscle, and organs in youth and supports repair, muscle maintenance, and metabolism afterward. It changes slowly, so it reflects average growth hormone activity better than a random growth hormone draw. Clinicians pair it with growth hormone, IGFBP-3, glucose, insulin, and thyroid markers.

**High / low:** low IGF-1 can signal growth hormone deficiency, malnutrition, kidney or liver disease, poorly controlled diabetes, or severe hypothyroidism, with low bone density, muscle loss, and fatigue. High IGF-1 can signal acromegaly, pregnancy, or high-protein overfeeding, and chronically high values link to insulin resistance and a slightly higher risk of some cancers. Function's age bands: 25–29, 150–220; 30–39, 130–190; 40–49, 120–180 ng/mL.

**Your result:** 190 ng/mL (May 20; not retested), in range at the top of the 30–39 band (130–190). Quest 53–331.

## Z Score — in range (May only)

**Measures:** how far the IGF-1 value sits from the average for the same age and sex, in standard deviations.

**Why it matters:** a Z score of 0 is average. Below −2.0 can suggest growth hormone deficiency; above +2.0 suggests growth hormone excess such as acromegaly or an excessive treatment dose. A score between −2 and +2 usually means normal growth hormone status, with exceptions such as small growth-hormone-secreting tumors or deficiency with normal IGF-1, which need more testing.

**Your result:** 0.6 SD (May 20; not retested), in range. Reference −2.0 to +2.0.
