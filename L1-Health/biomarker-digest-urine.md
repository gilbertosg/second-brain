# L1 — Biomarker Digest: Urine

**What this group shows:** the routine urinalysis: appearance, color, concentration (specific gravity, pH), chemistry dipsticks (glucose, ketones, protein, bilirubin, occult blood, nitrite, leukocyte esterase), and microscopy (bacteria, red cells, white cells, squamous cells, hyaline casts). All 16 are in range on both draws. Function files the random urine albumin under Kidney ([[L1-Health/biomarker-digest-kidney]]).

**How to read this page:** each entry paraphrases Function Health's explainer text from the raw file in its **Source** line. Results are as of Oct 2, 2026; [[L1-Health/function-health-registry]] stays the source of truth for values. All 16 entries are Tier 2, kept short because the raw explainers are short. Function's raw files for most of these carry no numeric optimal range; a negative or "none seen" result is the target.

---

## Appearance - Urine — in range

**Measures:** how clear or cloudy the urine looks.

**Why it matters:** Function's scale reads clear as possibly overhydrated, slightly clear as just right, cloudy as dehydration, urinary infection, kidney stones, blood, crystals, yeast, or parasites, and very cloudy (turbid) as protein or cellular material.

**Your result:** clear (May 19 and Oct 2), in range. Quest: clear.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/appearance-urine.md)

## Bacteria - Urine — in range

**Measures:** whether bacteria show under the microscope.

**Why it matters:** healthy urine is sterile, so bacteria usually indicate infection, especially with pain after urination, back pain, or blood in the urine. The test is mainly used to diagnose urinary tract infection.

**Your result:** none seen (May 19 and Oct 2), in range.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/bacteria-urine.md)

## Bilirubin - Urine — in range

**Measures:** bilirubin, the bile pigment from red-cell breakdown, in urine.

**Why it matters:** a healthy liver clears bilirubin through bile. Bilirubin in urine is often an early sign of liver disease.

**Your result:** negative (May 19 and Oct 2), in range. Blood bilirubin was also in range (0.5 mg/dL in Oct); see [[L1-Health/biomarker-digest-liver]].

## Color - Urine — in range

**Measures:** urine color from pale to dark, a visible hydration check.

**Why it matters:** fluids dilute the yellow pigments, so more water gives clearer urine. Color also shifts with B vitamins, laxatives, beets, rhubarb, and muscle damage (myoglobin gives a reddish tint). Persistent discoloration can help screen for kidney disorders or urinary tract infection.

**Your result:** yellow (May 19 and Oct 2), in range, which Function calls normal.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/color-urine.md)

## Glucose - Urine — in range

**Measures:** sugar in urine.

**Why it matters:** healthy urine holds too little glucose to detect. A very high-sugar meal can cause a temporary positive, but a positive result after a proper fast most often reflects uncontrolled diabetes, and Function advises reviewing blood glucose and HbA1c.

**Your result:** negative (May 19 and Oct 2), in range. Blood glucose was 93 mg/dL in Oct; see [[L1-Health/biomarker-digest-metabolic]].

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/glucose-urine.md)

## Hyaline Casts - Urine — in range

**Measures:** hyaline casts, protein cylinders seen under the microscope, the only cast type found in healthy urine.

**Why it matters:** too many suggest slow urine flow from strenuous exercise, diuretics, severe vomiting, or fever, and large amounts may point to kidney damage from reduced blood flow.

**Your result:** none seen (May 19 and Oct 2), in range.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/hyaline-casts-urine.md)

## Ketones - Urine — in range

**Measures:** ketones, the fuel the liver makes from fat when glucose is scarce.

**Why it matters:** ketones appear in urine during fasting or a ketogenic diet (70–80% fat, under 5% carbohydrate). Ketones with uncontrolled diabetes signal ketoacidosis, a serious imbalance most common in type 1 diabetes. This test measures urine only, not blood ketones.

**Your result:** negative (May 19 and Oct 2), in range.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/ketones-urine.md)

**Why it matters:** its presence usually means white cells entered the urinary tract in response to inflammation or infection.

## Nitrite - Urine — in range

**Measures:** nitrite, formed when bacteria in the urinary tract convert urine nitrate.

**Why it matters:** raised nitrite can signal a urinary tract infection. Not every bacterium makes nitrite, so an infection is still possible with a negative result.

**Your result:** negative (May 19 and Oct 2), in range.

## Occult Blood - Urine — in range

**Measures:** blood in urine (hematuria), including amounts too small to see.

**Why it matters:** the cause may be benign, but follow-up is usually needed. Function lists possible causes: menstruation, enlarged prostate, bladder infection, autoimmune kidney disease, kidney stone, bladder or kidney cancer, heavy exercise, and blood thinners. Red or pink urine can instead come from beets, rhubarb, berries, laxatives, or vitamins B2 and B12.

**Your result:** negative (May 19 and Oct 2), in range.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/occult-blood-urine.md)

## pH - Urine — in range

**Measures:** how acidic or alkaline the urine is.

**Why it matters:** urine pH reflects the balance of nutrients, electrolytes, and waste. Acidic urine goes with a diet high in fish, meat, and cheese or low in fruit and vegetables, with diabetic ketoacidosis, diarrhea, vomiting, or starvation. Alkaline urine goes with a diet high in fruit and vegetables, kidney stones, urinary infections, or kidney disorders. Testing screens for acid-base imbalance and helps monitor lung and kidney disease. Function states the body's natural pH is slightly alkaline at about 7.5 and lists 6.5–7.5 as optimal.

**Your result:** 8.0 (May 19) → 7.0 (Oct 2), in Quest range (5–8). May sat above Function's 6.5–7.5 band; Oct sits inside it.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/ph-urine.md)

## Protein - Urine — in range

**Measures:** protein in urine.

**Why it matters:** the kidneys' glomeruli normally keep protein in the blood, so protein in urine means they may be inflamed or damaged. A positive result should be monitored to see whether it resolves or worsens.

**Your result:** negative (May 19 and Oct 2), in range.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/protein-urine.md)

## Red Blood Cell (RBC) - Urine — in range

**Measures:** red cells seen under the microscope in urine.

**Why it matters:** red cells in urine (hematuria) can come from the kidney or anywhere in the urinary tract: ureters, bladder, prostate, or urethra. It is usually not life-threatening, but finding the cause matters because occasionally it is serious.

**Your result:** none seen (May 19 and Oct 2), in range. Quest ≤ 2 per high-power field.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/red-blood-cell-rbc-urine.md)

## Specific Gravity - Urine — in range

**Measures:** how concentrated the urine is.

**Why it matters:** it shows whether the kidneys are compensating for an abnormality. Under-concentrated urine can signal diabetes insipidus (thirst and large volumes of dilute urine). Over-concentrated urine usually means dehydration, and may reflect kidney dysfunction, so Function pairs it with blood creatinine. Some medications and herbal products can interfere with the test.

**Your result:** 1.005 (May 19) → 1.011 (Oct 2), in range. Quest and Function optimal 1.001–1.035.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/specific-gravity-urine.md)
