# L1 — Biomarker Digest: Pancreas

**What this group shows:** the two digestive enzymes the pancreas releases into blood when it is irritated or inflamed: amylase (starch) and lipase (fat). Both were measured once, on May 19, 2026, and sit in range.

**How to read this page:** each entry paraphrases Function Health's explainer text from the raw file in its **Source** line. Results are as of May 19, 2026; [[L1-Health/function-health-registry]] stays the source of truth for values. Both entries are Tier 2. Vault context: Function's ALT page lists lipase and amylase among the tests for a liver-enzyme work-up ([[L1-Health/liver-enzymes-alt-ast-oct2026]]).

## Amylase — in range (May only)

**Measures:** an enzyme made mainly by the pancreas and salivary glands that breaks starch into sugars.

**Why it matters:** it enters the blood when those glands are irritated or inflamed, so a high value can flag pancreatitis, salivary gland infection (mumps), or kidney dysfunction, since the kidneys clear it. Function reads it with lipase: high amylase with normal lipase points away from the pancreas toward salivary, gut, or kidney causes. Very high triglycerides (over 1,000 mg/dL) can falsely lower the reading even in acute pancreatitis.

**High / low:** high amylase is common in acute pancreatitis, which brings intense abdominal or back pain, nausea, vomiting, and fever. Low amylase can reflect chronic pancreatic damage, cystic fibrosis, or metabolic conditions such as metabolic syndrome, obesity, or diabetes. The usual range is 21–101 U/L.

**Your result:** 46 U/L (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest and Function optimal 21–101.

## Lipase — in range (May only)

**Measures:** the pancreatic enzyme that breaks dietary fat into absorbable forms, working with bile from the liver.

**Why it matters:** when the pancreas is damaged or inflamed, lipase leaks into the blood, so the level shows pancreatic health. A value three times the upper limit is highly specific for pancreatic injury, which helps separate pancreatic from non-pancreatic abdominal pain. Function reads it with amylase, stool fat tests, inflammation, liver, and blood sugar markers.

**High / low:** high lipase signals acute pancreatitis (gallstones, heavy alcohol use, high triglycerides) and, less often, intestinal obstruction, peptic ulcer, certain drugs, or early pancreatic cancer. Low lipase can mean pancreatic insufficiency (chronic pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis, advanced diabetes, advanced pancreatic cancer), with fatty stools, bloating, and nutrient deficiencies. The usual range is 7–60 U/L. High-fat meals, hydration, stress, infection, and some drugs move it.

**Your result:** 35 U/L (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest and Function optimal 7–60.
