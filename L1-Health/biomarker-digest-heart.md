**What this group shows:** the standard cholesterol numbers (total, LDL-C, HDL-C, non-HDL, triglycerides, ratios) and the advanced particle measures Function adds: how many LDL particles circulate (ApoB, LDL particle number), how big they are (peak size, pattern, small, medium), how many large HDL particles exist, and inherited risk (Lp(a)). ## Apolipoprotein B (ApoB) — in range (May only)

**Measures:** the protein on every LDL and VLDL particle. Because each particle carries one ApoB, the level counts the plaque-forming particles directly.

**Why it matters:** Function argues the number of ApoB particles predicts heart disease better than LDL cholesterol. Many small LDL particles compete for receptors, clear slowly, oxidize, and get taken up by immune cells that form foam cells and soft plaque, which can rupture. Insulin resistance raises liver VLDL output and so raises ApoB. Two people with the same LDL-C can carry very different ApoB. The ApoB/ApoA1 ratio (ApoA1 is an add-on test) compares harmful to protective particles.

**High / low:** high ApoB tracks with more small, dense LDL, insulin resistance, and atherosclerosis risk. Function's optimal range is 40–70 mg/dL, and below 50 for people with existing cardiovascular disease. Diet (refined carbohydrates, saturated fat), weight change, activity, medications, and metabolic health move it, while illness, stress, and inflammation cause short-term shifts. Function suggests rechecking ApoB, the ratio, triglycerides, LDL particle data, hs-CRP, and liver function every 6–12 months.

**Your result:** 56 mg/dL (May 19; not retested Oct 2), in range. Quest < 90; Function optimal 40–70. Tracked in the vault protocol (action trigger above 80 mg/dL); ApoB belongs on the next draw because LDL particle number rose.

## HDL Large — in range

**Measures:** the count of large, buoyant HDL particles, the protective kind.

**Why it matters:** HDL moves excess cholesterol back to the liver (reverse cholesterol transport). Two people with the same HDL-C can differ widely in risk depending on how many HDL particles are large. Function pairs it with the triglyceride-to-HDL ratio (ideal below 2.0) and the cholesterol-to-HDL ratio (2.5 or lower on this page), and mentions HDLfx pCAD and pCEC tests that measure HDL function.

**High / low:** low HDL Large points to chronic inflammation, insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, and higher cardiovascular risk. High HDL Large reflects healthy cholesterol transport. Function's optimal level is above 6,729 nmol/L. Refined carbohydrates and added sugar lower it; aerobic exercise, sleep, healthy weight, and omega-3 fats raise it.

**Your result:** 6,147 nmol/L (May 19) → 7,370 nmol/L (Oct 2), in range (below the threshold in May). Quest > 6,729.

## HDL-Cholesterol — in range

**Measures:** the cholesterol carried by HDL particles, by weight.

**Why it matters:** HDL clears cholesterol from the bloodstream, carries anti-inflammatory effects, and limits LDL oxidation. Low HDL-C goes with higher heart-disease risk even when LDL looks normal. Particle size and function matter as much as the number, and Function notes that raising HDL with drugs has not lowered risk, while aerobic exercise, less refined carbohydrate and sugar, weight loss, and not smoking help.

**High / low:** under 40 mg/dL for men is low and linked to higher risk; 40–59 is acceptable; 60 or higher is Function's optimal and protective range.

**Your result:** 66 mg/dL (May 19) → 63 mg/dL (Oct 2), in range. Quest ≥ 40; Function optimal ≥ 60.

## LDL Medium — 🔴 above range

**Measures:** the count of medium-sized LDL particles: smaller than the buoyant protective kind, larger than the small dense kind.

**Why it matters:** medium particles linger in circulation, oxidize, and add to arterial inflammation and plaque. They are less damaging than small dense particles but still add risk, and a high count can point to early metabolic dysfunction, especially alongside rising triglycerides, low HDL, or insulin resistance. Function reads it with LDL small, LDL particle number, LDL pattern, triglycerides, HDL, ApoB, and Lp(a).

**High / low:** high LDL Medium is an early warning sign that the body is packaging cholesterol differently, often from inflammation, poor blood sugar control, or early metabolic change. Low values reflect a healthier lipid profile. Function's optimal level is below 215 nmol/L. A meal high in refined carbohydrates can raise triglycerides and shift particles toward this class.

**Your result:** 203 nmol/L (May 19) → **294 nmol/L** (Oct 2), above range. Quest < 215; Function optimal < 215.

**Causes (Function):** a diet high in saturated and trans fat, refined carbohydrates, and added sugar; physical inactivity; chronic stress; insulin resistance; oxidative stress; chronic inflammation; gut dysbiosis; abdominal obesity; genetics such as familial hypercholesterolemia; hypothyroidism; liver dysfunction; toxin exposure.

**Follow-up (Function):** physician-guided plan; follow-up tests named are LDL particle number, LDL small, pattern, peak size, triglycerides, HDL, ApoB, hs-CRP, homocysteine, and Lp(a). Listed medication classes: statins, ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors, fibrates, prescription omega-3, SGLT2 inhibitors.

**Levers Function lists** *(its text, for physician discussion)*: Mediterranean or anti-inflammatory eating; at least 150 minutes of moderate exercise weekly with strength training; gradual weight loss; limiting alcohol; gut-health support; time-restricted eating (no food 3 hours before bed, 12–16 hours overnight). Foods to limit: refined carbohydrates, added sugar, trans fats, processed meat. Supplements it names: omega-3 (1,000–3,000 mg), soluble fiber (β-glucan, konjac glucomannan), CoQ10, plant sterols (see the raw file for doses). Function advises a clinician review before any supplement.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/ldl-medium.md)

## LDL Particle Number — 🔴 above range

**Measures:** how many LDL particles circulate (LDL-P), as opposed to how much cholesterol they carry.

**Why it matters:** more particles mean more chances to enter artery walls, oxidize, and build plaque, even when LDL-C looks normal. Function calls LDL-P often a better predictor of cardiovascular events than LDL-C, and notes it commonly rises with insulin resistance, prediabetes, type 2 diabetes, and visceral fat. Chronic inflammation makes the particles more likely to oxidize.

**High / low:** above the range (typically above about 1,000 nmol/L) means too many particles, with higher risk of atherosclerosis, heart attack, and stroke. Within range usually reflects healthier lipid metabolism. Function's optimal level is below 1,138 nmol/L. It advises checking pattern, LDL small, and peak size next. Diet quality, exercise, stress, blood sugar control, and sleep move it.

**Your result:** 1,033 nmol/L (May 19) → **1,309 nmol/L** (Oct 2), above range. Quest < 1,138; Function optimal < 1,138.

**Causes (Function):** the same list as LDL Medium: saturated and trans fat, refined carbohydrates and sugar, sedentary life, chronic stress, insulin resistance (named a major driver), oxidative stress, inflammation, gut dysbiosis, abdominal obesity, familial hypercholesterolemia, hypothyroidism, liver dysfunction, environmental toxins.

**Follow-up (Function):** physician-guided plan; follow-up tests named are LDL small, pattern, peak size, triglycerides, HDL, ApoB, hs-CRP, homocysteine, and Lp(a). Medication classes listed: statins, ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors, fibrates, prescription omega-3, SGLT2 inhibitors.

**Levers Function lists** *(its text, for physician discussion)*: the same set as LDL Medium: Mediterranean eating, 150 minutes of weekly exercise plus strength training, gradual weight loss, limiting alcohol, gut support, time-restricted eating. Foods to limit: refined carbohydrates, added sugar, trans fats, processed meat. Supplements it names: omega-3 (1,000–3,000 mg), soluble fiber, CoQ10, plant sterols. Function advises a clinician review before any supplement.

## LDL Pattern — in range

**Measures:** whether most LDL particles are large and buoyant (pattern A) or small and dense (pattern B).

**Why it matters:** pattern B goes with higher ApoB, lower HDL, higher triglycerides, and a tendency toward insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes, because small dense particles oxidize and stick to artery walls more easily. Pattern A is the protective result. Function reads it beside ApoB, LDL small, LDL-P, triglycerides, HDL, and Lp(a), and suggests pCAD and pCEC testing if pattern B appears.

**High / low:** pattern A is optimal. A shift toward B follows refined carbohydrates and added sugar; healthy fats, fiber, exercise, and better insulin sensitivity shift it back over weeks to months.

**Your result:** pattern A on both draws (May 19 and Oct 2), in range.

## LDL Peak Size — in range

**Measures:** the most common LDL particle size in the blood, in angstroms.

**Why it matters:** smaller particles slip into artery walls more easily and trigger inflammation. Function reads it with LDL particle number, pattern, triglycerides, HDL, ApoB, and Lp(a): a small peak size plus a high particle count or high ApoB means more harmful particles circulating.

**High / low:** below about 217.4 Å suggests dominant small dense particles and higher risk with insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, and inflammation. Above 222.9 Å means larger, buoyant particles and lower risk. Function's optimal level is above 222.9 Å. Refined carbohydrates can raise triglycerides and shrink the particles; poor sleep and stress add inflammation that lowers it.

**Your result:** 222.4 Å (May 19) → 225.3 Å (Oct 2), in range (just under the threshold in May). Quest > 222.9.

## LDL Small — 🔴 above range

**Measures:** the count of the smallest, densest LDL particles.

**Why it matters:** Function calls small LDL particles the major driver of cardiovascular disease. They enter artery walls, oxidize, and trigger inflammation, so a high count raises risk even when total LDL-C is normal. High triglycerides with low HDL usually mean many small particles, a pattern common in insulin resistance, prediabetes, and metabolic syndrome. A high count can signal increased cardiovascular risk, insulin resistance, and chronic inflammation.

**High / low:** high means more risk from plaque-forming particles and often reflects oxidative stress, blood sugar problems, or low-grade inflammation, usually without symptoms. Low is a good sign, though inflammation and insulin resistance still need attention. Function's optimal level is below 142 nmol/L.

**Your result:** 124 nmol/L (May 19) → **153 nmol/L** (Oct 2), above range. Quest < 142; Function optimal < 142.

**Causes (Function):** insulin resistance (named the main driver), a diet high in saturated and trans fat and refined carbohydrates, physical inactivity, chronic stress, oxidative stress, inflammation, gut dysbiosis, abdominal obesity, familial hypercholesterolemia, hypothyroidism, liver dysfunction.

**Follow-up (Function):** physician-guided plan; follow-up tests named are LDL particle number, LDL medium, HDL Large, pattern, peak size, ApoB, and hs-CRP. Medication classes listed: statins, ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors, fibrates, prescription omega-3, SGLT2 inhibitors.

**Levers Function lists** *(its text, for physician discussion)*: Mediterranean eating, 150 minutes of weekly exercise plus strength training, gradual weight loss, limiting alcohol, gut support, time-restricted eating. Foods to limit: refined carbohydrates, added sugar, trans fats, processed meat. Supplements it names: omega-3, soluble fiber, CoQ10, plant sterols. Function advises a clinician review before any supplement.

## LDL-Cholesterol — in range

**Measures:** the cholesterol carried by LDL particles (calculated).

**Why it matters:** LDL deposits cholesterol on artery walls when too much circulates, and plaque narrows the vessels over time. Function stresses that LDL-C alone misleads: particle size and number, the triglyceride-to-HDL ratio (ideal about 1:1; above 2:1 raises risk), hs-CRP, and insulin give the real pattern. High LDL with high triglycerides and low HDL can suggest insulin resistance before blood sugar rises.

**High / low:** high LDL-C may signal plaque risk and metabolic or inflammatory trouble. Low LDL-C is uncommon and can accompany genetic conditions, severe liver disease, or an overactive thyroid. Function's optimal range is 20–70 mg/dL; under 100 is generally acceptable, under 70 for higher-risk people, and under 55 for very high-risk people. A fatty meal the night before, chronic stress, weight change, hormones, and thyroid problems can move it.

**Your result:** 76 mg/dL (May 19) → 82 mg/dL (Oct 2), in Quest range (< 100) and above Function's optimal band (20–70).

## Lipoprotein (a) — in range (May only)

**Measures:** an inherited cholesterol particle: an LDL particle with an extra sticky protein, apolipoprotein(a).

**Why it matters:** high Lp(a) independently raises the risk of heart attack, ischemic stroke, aortic valve narrowing, and clotting, even when other cholesterol numbers look normal. It stays stable across life, so Function measures it once at the first annual test to establish inherited risk. Repeat testing is useful only near a decision threshold (about 125 nmol/L) or after starting lipid-lowering drugs.

**High / low:** elevated Lp(a) means higher lifelong risk and can change how aggressively a clinician approaches prevention and family screening; diet and exercise change it little. A low result points to lower inherited risk. Function's optimal level is below 50 nmol/L. Acute illness, inflammation, hormone changes, and kidney or liver disease can shift it temporarily.

**Your result:** < 10 nmol/L (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest < 75; Function optimal 0–50.

## Non-HDL Cholesterol — in range

**Measures:** all cholesterol in plaque-forming particles: LDL plus VLDL and IDL (calculated as total minus HDL).

**Why it matters:** it captures the harmful cholesterol that LDL-C alone misses, and Function calls it a better predictor of heart disease than LDL-C. High values tend to appear alongside raised triglycerides, weak HDL, insulin resistance, or diabetes; low values suggest lower risk.

**High / low:** Function's optimal level is below 100 mg/dL. Elevated values can point to diet, insulin resistance, inflammation, or liver dysfunction. Refined carbohydrates raise triglycerides, which sit inside this number.

**Your result:** 91 mg/dL (May 19) → 99 mg/dL (Oct 2), in range, just under Function's 100 ceiling. Quest < 130.

## Total Cholesterol — in range

**Measures:** all cholesterol circulating in the blood: HDL, LDL, and the cholesterol in VLDL and IDL.

**Why it matters:** it gives a starting view of heart and metabolic health but little on its own. Function's examples show a person with 300 mg/dL and mostly large buoyant particles at low risk, and a person with 150 mg/dL and mostly small dense particles at higher risk. It reads the number with lipoprotein fractionation, triglycerides, HDL, ApoB, and Lp(a), and looks for insulin resistance, an underactive thyroid, liver dysfunction, or inflammation behind a high value.

**High / low:** Function's optimal range is 125–199 mg/dL for men and women. Elevated total cholesterol can point to diet, insulin resistance, inflammation, thyroid, or liver causes. Meals high in refined carbohydrates raise triglycerides, which sit inside this number.

**Your result:** 157 mg/dL (May 19) → 162 mg/dL (Oct 2), in range. Quest < 200; Function optimal 125–199.

## Total Cholesterol / HDL Ratio — in range

**Measures:** total cholesterol divided by HDL cholesterol (calculated).

**Why it matters:** the ratio shows the balance between plaque-forming and protective cholesterol and describes heart risk better than total cholesterol or LDL-C alone. A high ratio with small dense LDL or high ApoB means more risk even when total cholesterol is normal. Function pairs it with the triglyceride-to-HDL ratio (ideal below 2.0).

**High / low:** a high ratio means more cholesterol available to build up in artery walls, especially with low HDL, high LDL, inflammation, or insulin resistance. A low ratio reflects a healthier balance. Function's optimal ratio is below 3.5.

**Your result:** 2.4 (May 19) → 2.6 (Oct 2), in range. Quest < 5; Function optimal < 3.5.

## Triglycerides — in range (above Function optimal)

**Measures:** the main fat stored for energy, in the blood.

**Why it matters:** the body turns surplus calories, especially from carbohydrate and sugar, into triglycerides. Levels rise with insulin resistance, overeating relative to activity, obesity, fatty liver, and inflammation, and they respond quickly to diet and exercise. High triglycerides are a core part of metabolic syndrome. The triglyceride-to-HDL ratio (ideal about 1:1; above 2:1 raises risk; 5:1 is strongly linked to heart disease, insulin resistance, and prediabetes) adds to the picture.

**High / low:** high triglycerides link to insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, obesity, fatty liver, and cardiovascular risk. Over 500 mg/dL raises pancreatitis risk. Under 40 can appear with very low-fat diets, malnutrition, or an overactive thyroid. Function's optimal range is 30–70 mg/dL for men.

**Your result:** 74 mg/dL (May 19) → 91 mg/dL (Oct 2), in Quest range (< 150) and above Function's optimal band (30–70) at the Oct draw.
