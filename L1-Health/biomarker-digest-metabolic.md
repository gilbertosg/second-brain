# L1 — Biomarker Digest: Metabolic

**What this group shows:** how the body handles sugar and energy: fasting glucose, the three-month average (HbA1c), fasting insulin, the fat-cell hunger signal leptin, and uric acid from purine breakdown. ## Glucose — in range

**Measures:** fasting blood sugar, the body's main fuel.

**Why it matters:** carbohydrate becomes glucose, insulin moves it into cells, and glucagon releases stored glycogen when it falls. The liver turns surplus glucose into triglycerides and belly fat. Swings from refined carbohydrate or a faltering insulin response cause fatigue and mood swings and, over time, damage blood vessels and nerves. Function reads glucose with insulin, HbA1c, and C-peptide to catch insulin resistance early.

**High / low:** low glucose can follow skipped meals, excess insulin or medication, or hormone imbalance and causes shakiness, sweating, and confusion. High glucose often points to insulin resistance or diabetes, with thirst, frequent urination, and fatigue. Function's optimal fasting range is 70–85 mg/dL; it says 80–94 carries the lowest all-cause mortality risk and that research ties any fasting glucose above 87 mg/dL to higher cardiovascular risk. Meals, movement, stress, sleep, illness, and medications move it through the day. Function suggests rechecking fasting glucose every three to six months.

**Your result:** 100 mg/dL (May 19, above the Quest limit) → 93 mg/dL (Oct 2), in Quest range (65–99) and above both Function's optimal band (70–85) and its 87 mg/dL cardiovascular marker.

## Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) — in range

**Measures:** the share of hemoglobin carrying attached glucose, which reports average blood sugar over the past two to three months.

**Why it matters:** it acts as a long-term report card for blood sugar control and also reflects inflammation, metabolic health, and the risk of diabetes, heart disease, and accelerated aging. Function reads it with fasting glucose, fasting insulin, an insulin resistance score, and an oral glucose tolerance test, and uses it to catch metabolic syndrome and diabetes early.

**High / low:** elevated HbA1c means blood sugar has stayed high, with poorer control and higher complication risk. Very low values warrant a check for hypoglycemia. Function's optimal range is 4.8–5.5%. Stress, illness, diet or exercise changes, and hydration move it a little. It suggests an annual HbA1c with fasting glucose every three to six months.

**Your result:** 5.2% (May 19) → 5.3% (Oct 2), in Quest range (< 5.7) and inside Function's optimal band (4.8–5.5). The vault's protocol trigger is 5.3% (">" in the protocol, "≥" in the scorecard), so the Oct value sits on the line; see [[L1-Health/blood-analysis-function-oct2026]].

## Insulin — in range (above Function optimal)

**Measures:** fasting insulin, the hormone that moves glucose into muscle, liver, and fat cells and suppresses liver glucose output.

**Why it matters:** fasting insulin can climb for years before glucose rises, because the pancreas compensates for cells that respond poorly. That makes it an early signal of insulin resistance, prediabetes, and chronic metabolic stress. Function links chronically high insulin to cardiovascular disease, abnormal cell growth, and Alzheimer's disease. Prediabetes starts at fasting glucose of 100–125 mg/dL and diabetes above 126.

**High / low:** elevated fasting insulin points to insulin resistance, obesity, metabolic syndrome, and higher risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease. Low insulin can indicate the pancreas is not producing enough, as in type 1 diabetes. Function's optimal level is below 5 uIU/mL, with some practitioners targeting 2–5.

**Your result:** 10.9 uIU/mL (May 19) → 12.5 uIU/mL (Oct 2), in Quest range (≤ 18.4) and 2.5 times the top of Function's optimal band (5).

**Causes (Function):** insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome, a diet heavy in refined carbohydrates and sugar, chronic stress, sleep deprivation, abdominal obesity, a sedentary life, chronic inflammation, gut dysbiosis, oxidative stress, cortisol excess or excess androgens, magnesium, chromium, and vitamin D shortfalls, genetics, and rarely an insulin-producing tumor. Listed drugs: corticosteroids, antipsychotics, beta-blockers, oral contraceptives, and others.

**Follow-up (Function):** confirm with an insulin resistance score or an oral glucose tolerance test with insulin readings plus HbA1c; assess lipids, liver function (fatty liver), hs-CRP, thyroid, and C-peptide; physician-guided treatment may include metformin or GLP-1 receptor agonists; repeat insulin, glucose, and related markers to judge progress.

## Leptin — in range (May only)

**Measures:** a hormone made by fat cells that reports stored energy to the brain.

**Why it matters:** leptin works like a fuel gauge: full stores quiet hunger and low stores raise it. In obesity and leptin resistance the brain stops hearing the signal, which promotes overeating, belly fat, and insulin resistance. Function reads it with testosterone, fasting insulin, glucose, and hs-CRP and notes testosterone interacts with fat tissue and leptin signaling.

**High / low:** high leptin goes with belly fat, metabolic syndrome, low testosterone, insulin resistance, and inflammation. Low leptin goes with low body fat; in extreme calorie restriction it can lower testosterone and muscle. Ranges depend on BMI: 18–25, 0.3–13.4 ng/mL; 25–30, 1.8–19.9 ng/mL. Leptin rises at night, falls in the morning, and drops after a day of fasting.

**Your result:** 4.5 ng/mL (May 19; not retested), in range for the BMI 25–30 band. Quest 1.8–19.9.

## Uric Acid — in range (above Function optimal)

**Measures:** the waste product of purine breakdown, filtered out by the kidneys.

**Why it matters:** purines come from the body's own cells and from red meat, seafood, and sugary drinks. Too much production or too little kidney clearance raises levels. Function reads uric acid beside glucose, insulin, HbA1c, and creatinine as a window into metabolic balance and kidney efficiency.

**High / low:** high uric acid can drive inflammation and crystal formation with gout, kidney stones, and cardiovascular risk. Low levels can reflect malnutrition, genetic purine disorders, or kidney dysfunction. Function's optimal range for men is 4.0–5.5 mg/dL. Diet, hydration, diuretics or aspirin, alcohol, exercise, fasting, and sudden weight loss move it.

**Your result:** 6.3 mg/dL (May 19; not retested), in Quest range (4.0–8.0) and above Function's optimal band (4.0–5.5).
