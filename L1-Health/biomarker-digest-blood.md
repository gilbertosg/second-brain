# L1 — Biomarker Digest: Blood

**What this group shows:** the complete blood count's red-cell and platelet side (hematocrit, hemoglobin, RBC count, MCV, MCH, MCHC, RDW, platelet count, MPV) plus blood type (ABO group and Rh factor). The white-cell side sits in [[L1-Health/biomarker-digest-immune-regulation]]. Every red-cell and platelet marker is in range at both draws.

**How to read this page:** each entry paraphrases Function Health's explainer text from the raw file in its **Source** line. Results are as of Oct 2, 2026 unless noted; [[L1-Health/function-health-registry]] stays the source of truth for values. All 11 entries are Tier 2. Vault context: [[L1-Health/blood-analysis]] (the MCH pattern from 2023–2024) and [[L1-Health/biomarker-reference]].

## ABO Group — recorded (May only)

**Measures:** your ABO blood group, from the A and B antigens on red cells (the Rh factor is a separate entry).

**Why it matters:** blood type decides which donor blood is compatible in a transfusion; incompatible blood can clump cells and be fatal. Knowing it also tells you whether a rare type makes you a needed donor.

**Your result:** type A (May 19). No range applies.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/abo-group.md)

## Hematocrit — in range

**Measures:** the percentage of blood volume made up of red blood cells.

**Why it matters:** it shows how well blood carries oxygen and also reflects hydration. Function reads it with hemoglobin and RBC count: all three low points to anemia, all three high points to dehydration or conditions that overproduce red cells (altitude, chronic lung disease).

**High / low:** low hematocrit goes with anemia from iron, B12, or folate shortage, chronic disease, or blood loss, with fatigue, dizziness, and breathlessness. High hematocrit suggests dehydration, lung disease, altitude, or polycythemia with higher clot risk. Function treats about 40–45% for men as a practical target because both ends of the range link to higher mortality. Hydration, exercise, altitude, and time of day shift it.

**Your result:** 46.8% (May 19) → 45.3% (Oct 2), in Quest range (39.4–51.1) and just above Function's 40–45% band at the Oct draw.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/hematocrit.md)

## Hemoglobin — in range

**Measures:** the oxygen-carrying protein inside red blood cells.

**Why it matters:** hemoglobin delivers oxygen from the lungs to tissues and helps remove carbon dioxide, so it sets energy and organ function. Function reads it with hematocrit, RBC count, and ferritin: low hemoglobin with low ferritin means iron deficiency anemia; high hemoglobin with high hematocrit suggests dehydration or polycythemia.

**High / low:** low hemoglobin means anemia from iron deficiency, inflammation, or blood loss, with fatigue and poor concentration. High hemoglobin can mean dehydration, chronic lung disease, or polycythemia vera, with clot risk and heart strain. Function's optimal band is 11.1–15.9 g/dL, tending toward the upper end for oxygen delivery. Hydration, meals, activity, altitude, stress, and infection move it.

**Your result:** 16.5 g/dL (May 19) → 15.8 g/dL (Oct 2), in Quest range (13.2–17.1); inside Function's band in Oct, above it in May.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/hemoglobin.md)

## Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin (MCH) — in range

**Measures:** the average hemoglobin inside one red blood cell.

**Why it matters:** MCH shows how well new red cells are made. Read with hemoglobin, hematocrit, MCV, ferritin, and B12: iron deficiency gives low MCH and low MCV; B12 or folate shortage gives high MCH with large cells. Pairing it with hs-CRP can show inflammation hiding an iron problem.

**High / low:** low MCH suggests iron deficiency anemia, chronic blood loss, or thalassemia. High MCH suggests B12 or folate shortage, liver disease, hypothyroidism, heavy alcohol use, certain drugs, or a bone-marrow disorder. Function lists 26–33 pg as desirable. Red cells live about 120 days, so nutrient changes show up weeks later.

**Your result:** 32.9 pg (May 19) → 32.6 pg (Oct 2), in range, just under the 33.0 upper limit. The vault recorded MCH above range on the three 2023–2024 draws; the last two draws sit in range. Quest 27.0–33.0; Function optimal 26–33.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/mean-corpuscular-hemoglobin-mch.md)

## Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin Concentration (MCHC) — in range

**Measures:** how densely hemoglobin is packed in red cells, adjusted for cell size.

**Why it matters:** where MCH counts hemoglobin per cell, MCHC shows its concentration. Low MCHC with low MCV points to iron deficiency anemia; high MCHC with normal MCV can suggest a rare condition such as hereditary spherocytosis.

**High / low:** low MCHC means thin hemoglobin loading from iron deficiency, chronic blood loss, thalassemia, or chronic disease, with fatigue, cold sensitivity, and pallor. High MCHC can reflect inherited disorders, hemolytic anemia, severe dehydration, or liver disease, but Function says it more often reflects lab artifact such as fatty blood, cell breakage, or clumping. Function's optimal range is 31.5–35.7 g/dL. Hydration, meals, and sample handling move it.

**Your result:** 35.3 g/dL (May 19) → 34.9 g/dL (Oct 2), in range. Quest 31.6–35.4; Function optimal 31.5–35.7.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/mean-corpuscular-hemoglobin-concentration-mchc.md)

## Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV) — in range

**Measures:** the average size of red blood cells.

**Why it matters:** size sorts types of anemia. Read with MCH and MCHC: small cells with low hemoglobin point to iron deficiency; large cells with high MCH point to B12 or folate deficiency.

**High / low:** high MCV (macrocytic) can come from B12 or folate shortage, hypothyroidism, chronic alcohol use, or liver disease, with fatigue, weakness, and brain fog. Low MCV (microcytic) comes from iron deficiency or inherited conditions such as thalassemia. Function's text calls 85–90 fL optimal, slightly narrower than the conventional 80–100, and flags values above 95 fL; the exported band reads 85–95. Hydration, nutrient levels, alcohol, inflammation, and liver or marrow health move it.

**Your result:** 93.2 fL (May 19) → 93.4 fL (Oct 2), in Quest range (81.4–101.7), above Function's text-stated 85–90 band and under its 95 flag.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/mean-corpuscular-volume-mcv.md)

## Mean Platelet Volume (MPV) — in range

**Measures:** the average size of platelets, the cells that clot blood and help repair vessels.

**Why it matters:** platelets also regulate inflammation and live only 7–10 days, so MPV shows how fast bone marrow turns them over. Function reads it with platelet count, RBC, and WBC for clotting risk, inflammation, and marrow health.

**High / low:** low MPV means smaller, older platelets and possible under-production from marrow problems, long-term inflammation, or genetic conditions; low MPV with a low count can mean marrow suppression from infection, chemotherapy, or B12 or folate shortage. High MPV means larger platelets from fast turnover in inflammation or recovery from low counts, and larger platelets can be more active, raising clot and heart risk. Function's optimal range is 7.2–11.7 fL. Stress, exercise, infection, hydration, and sample handling move it.

**Your result:** 10.7 fL (May 19) → 10.9 fL (Oct 2), in range. Quest 7.5–12.5; Function optimal 7.2–11.7.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/mean-platelet-volume-mpv.md)

## Platelet Count — in range

**Measures:** the number of platelets per microliter of blood.

**Why it matters:** platelets stop bleeding, repair vessels, and influence inflammation. Counts that run too high or low can point to chronic inflammation, infection, marrow disorders, or hidden nutrient gaps, and they inform clotting risk. Function reads the count with MPV and the other CBC markers.

**High / low:** high platelets (thrombocytosis) follow inflammation, infection, iron deficiency, or some cancers and can raise clot risk. Low platelets (thrombocytopenia) follow B12 or folate shortage, liver disease, autoimmune disorders, or marrow problems and raise bleeding and bruising risk. Function's optimal range for men 64 or younger is 141,000–362,000 per microliter. Intense exercise, acute infection, and some drugs move the count.

**Your result:** 193 Thousand/uL (May 19) → 206 Thousand/uL (Oct 2), in range. Quest 140–400; Function optimal 141–362.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/platelet-count.md)

## Red Blood Cell (RBC) Count — in range

**Measures:** the number of red blood cells.

**Why it matters:** RBCs carry oxygen from the lungs to tissues and remove carbon dioxide. Function reads the count with hemoglobin and hematocrit to identify anemia or polycythemia and decide on further testing.

**High / low:** a low count means anemia from iron deficiency, chronic illness, or marrow problems, with fatigue, weakness, and dizziness. A high count (polycythemia) thickens blood and raises clot, heart-strain, and stroke risk, and it can come from dehydration, lung conditions, or marrow disorders. Function's optimal range for men is 3.77–5.28 million per microliter. Hydration, altitude, exercise, minor infection, stress, and some drugs move it.

**Your result:** 5.02 Million/uL (May 19) → 4.85 Million/uL (Oct 2), in range. Quest 4.20–5.80; Function optimal 3.77–5.28.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/red-blood-cell-rbc-count.md)

## Red Cell Distribution Width (RDW) — in range

**Measures:** how much red cell sizes vary.

**Why it matters:** uniform cells travel and carry oxygen smoothly. A wide spread can be an early sign of nutrient gaps, inflammation, or broader imbalance. Read with MCV, hemoglobin, and ferritin: high RDW with low MCV suggests iron deficiency; high RDW with high MCV suggests B12 or folate deficiency.

**High / low:** high RDW can reflect iron, B12, or folate deficiency, liver disease, kidney dysfunction, or chronic inflammation, and persistent elevation links to cardiovascular risk through oxidative stress and inflammation. Low RDW is rarely a concern and has associated with lower mortality. Function's optimal range is 11.7–15.4%. Illness recovery, inflammation, nutrient shortfalls, hydration, and new supplements move it.

**Your result:** 13.5% (May 19) → 12.8% (Oct 2), in range. Quest 11–15; Function optimal 11.7–15.4.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/red-cell-distribution-width-rdw.md)

## Rhesus (Rh) Factor — recorded (May only)

**Measures:** whether your red cells carry the Rh protein (positive) or lack it (negative).

**Why it matters:** the main concern is fertility and pregnancy. If a mother is Rh-negative and her baby is Rh-positive, her immune system can make antibodies against the baby's blood, which Function says can be treated with immune globulin (RhoGAM). Knowing both partners' types shows whether the risk exists.

**Your result:** Rh(D) positive (May 19). No range applies.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/rhesus-rh-factor.md)
