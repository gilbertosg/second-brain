## Trend Analysis

### Weight Cycle Pattern

The data reveals a clear **annual weight cycling pattern**:

| Period | Weight Range | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 (spring) | 85–87 kg | Lowest recorded — marathon training period |
| 2024 (full year) | 89–94 kg | Stable ~90–91 kg mid-year, spikes at W18 (93.9) |
| 2025 (early) | 94–95 kg | **Peak: 95.1 kg** W2 — highest recorded |
| 2025 (summer-fall) | 88–91 kg | Best 2025 phase: W38 at 88.7 kg |
| 2025-2026 transition | 93–95 kg | Regained weight from Q4 2025 |
| 2026 (current) | 92–95 kg | Slight decline W9→W17, but still elevated |

### Body Fat Trend

- **Best recorded:** 24.8% (2024 W12)
- **Current estimated:** ~27% (based on W5 2026)
- **Target:** 20%
- **Gap:** ~7 percentage points
- **Relationship:** To reach 20% body fat while preserving SMM (~37 kg), total weight needs to fall to approximately **84–86 kg** — consistent with the −8 kg OKR target.

### Skeletal Muscle Mass (SMM) — Stable

SMM has held remarkably stable at **35.7–37.4 kg** across the entire 2024–2026 period. Weight fluctuations are driven by fat mass changes, not muscle loss. This is a positive finding: the body composition architecture is intact. Weight loss from here should reduce fat, not muscle.

### Body Water — Inverse Relationship

Body water percentage is higher (54–55%) when weight is lower. This is expected: fat tissue holds less water than lean tissue. Current 53% water at higher weight confirms elevated fat mass.

## 2026 OKR Projection

**To reach 8 kg loss by end of 2026:**
- Starting weight (W1 2026): 92.4 kg → Target: ~84.4 kg
- Required rate: ~8 kg over ~52 weeks = ~0.15 kg/week average
- The body has reached 85 kg before (2023) and sustained 88–90 kg for extended periods (2024 summer, 2025 summer)
- **The goal is achievable but requires active fat loss, not just weight maintenance.**

**Body fat 20% requirement:**
- At 20% body fat with current SMM (~37 kg), total weight ≈ 46.25 kg fat-free mass / 0.80 = **~84–86 kg**
- This directly aligns the weight and body fat KRs — they are the same target expressed differently.
