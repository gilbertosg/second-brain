# L1 — Biomarker Digest: Male Health

**What this group shows:** the testosterone axis (total and free testosterone, SHBG, LH, FSH), its precursors and conversion products (androstenedione, DHT, estradiol, progesterone), prolactin, and the prostate markers (PSA total, free, percent-free). All 13 are in range at Quest; free and total testosterone sit well below Function's optimal bands.

**How to read this page:** each entry paraphrases Function Health's explainer text from the raw file in its **Source** line. Results are as of Oct 2, 2026; [[L1-Health/function-health-registry]] stays the source of truth for values. Tier 1 entries (free and total testosterone, SHBG) note where Function's text bears on vault claims. Function's raw files for these three carry no Causes or Follow-up sections. Vault context: [[L1-Health/blood-analysis]] (hormone trends) and [[02-Indexes/topic_maps/health-performance-crossmap]] (Free T chain, including the SHBG correction).

## Androstenedione — in range (May only)

**Measures:** a hormone made in the adrenal glands and testes that the body converts into testosterone or estrogen.

**Why it matters:** it is a precursor in the androgen pathway, so it reflects how the body balances sex-hormone production alongside DHEA, testosterone, and estradiol. Function lists abnormal values as a clue to adrenal disorders, Cushing's syndrome, or metabolic problems tied to obesity and inflammation, and as a way to separate adrenal from testicular causes of imbalance.

**High / low:** low values appear in adrenal insufficiency or hypogonadism and can bring low energy, weakness, and low libido. High values suggest adrenal overactivity or androgen-secreting tumors and may add acne or hair loss. The reference range is 40–190 ng/dL; Function suggests the middle of the range is the best target when other hormones balance. It follows a daily rhythm, peaking in the morning.

**Your result:** 75 ng/dL (May 20; not retested), in range. Quest 40–190.

## Dihydrotestosterone (DHT) — in range (May only)

**Measures:** DHT, a potent androgen made from testosterone by the enzyme 5-alpha reductase in skin, prostate, and hair follicles.

**Why it matters:** DHT binds androgen receptors more strongly than testosterone and drives facial hair, muscle, and reproductive tissue. Excess can contribute to male-pattern baldness, acne, and benign prostate enlargement; low levels can impair sexual development and reduce libido. It matters most when someone uses testosterone therapy, because excess conversion adds side effects.

**High / low:** the reference range is 12–65 ng/dL, and Function suggests the middle of the range suits most adults. Blood levels do not always match tissue effect, since DHT acts differently across tissues and people respond with different sensitivity.

**Your result:** 15 ng/dL (May 20; not retested), in range, near the bottom of the 12–65 range. Quest 12–65.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/dihydrotestosterone-dht.md)

## Estradiol (E2) — in range

**Measures:** the main estrogen, which men make mostly by converting testosterone with the enzyme aromatase in fat, muscle, and brain.

**Why it matters:** in men it supports bone strength, metabolism, sex drive, and brain and heart health. Testosterone and estradiol move like a seesaw, and SHBG carries both. The liver clears excess estradiol, so alcohol, poor diet, or toxins can disturb balance. Function says holding estradiol near its ideal helps insulin sensitivity and limits fat gain.

**High / low:** high estradiol points to extra aromatase activity from obesity, insulin resistance, or alcohol, and can bring weight gain, breast tissue growth, mood swings, and heart risk. Low estradiol can weaken bone, cause joint pain, and reduce sexual function, often with low testosterone or estrogen-blocking drugs. Function lists 10–40 pg/mL as the usual range with about 30–35 pg/mL as the target.

**Your result:** 39 pg/mL (May 19) → 35 pg/mL (Oct 2), in range at the top of Quest's limit (≤ 39); Function optimal 30–35.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/estradiol-e2.md)

## Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH) — in range

**Measures:** a pituitary hormone that signals the testes to make sperm and supports testosterone production.

**Why it matters:** FSH read together with LH and testosterone shows whether a problem sits in the testes or in the brain's signaling, which guides evaluation of fertility, low testosterone, and sexual dysfunction.

**High / low:** high FSH suggests the testes respond poorly to the brain's signals (testicular failure, injury, genetic disorders, chemotherapy, radiation, infection). Low FSH points to the pituitary or hypothalamus, as in hypogonadism or pituitary disorders.

**Your result:** 3.2 mIU/mL (May 19) → 3.1 mIU/mL (Oct 2), in range. Quest 1.4–12.8; Function optimal 1.5–12.4.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/follicle-stimulating-hormone-fsh.md)

## Luteinizing Hormone (LH) — in range

**Measures:** a pituitary hormone that tells the testes to make testosterone.

**Why it matters:** LH is a messenger in the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis. With testosterone and FSH it separates primary hypogonadism (testes not responding) from secondary hypogonadism (pituitary or hypothalamus not signaling).

**High / low:** elevated LH suggests primary testicular failure (Klinefelter syndrome, testicular injury). Low LH suggests a pituitary or hypothalamic problem such as a tumor or chronic stress. LH is released in pulses, so the time of day matters, and stress, sleep, exercise, nutrient status, illness, and medications move it. Function treats the lab's reference range as the optimal range.

**Your result:** 1.4 mIU/mL (May 19, just below range) → 3.5 mIU/mL (Oct 2), in range. Quest 1.5–9.3.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/luteinizing-hormone-lh.md)

## Progesterone — in range (Oct only)

**Measures:** a steroid made mostly in the adrenals that serves as a building block for testosterone and cortisol.

**Why it matters:** in men it supports reproductive, cognitive, and metabolic balance and rises and falls with cortisol during stress. Brain-active metabolites of progesterone modulate GABA receptors, which Function links to mood, anxiety, and sleep. Low progesterone can leave estrogen relatively dominant, which Function ties to midsection weight gain and prostate issues.

**High / low:** low progesterone may accompany low testosterone and altered LH/FSH, with fatigue, low libido, and mood changes. High values mostly occur with exogenous progesterone-like drugs and then dampen testosterone; unexplained high values in men are uncommon. Function suggests roughly 0.2–0.9 ng/mL as optimal, with the high end of normal (about 1.0–1.4) linked to blood sugar problems.

**Your result:** < 0.1 ng/mL (Oct 2), in range at Quest (0–0.2) and below the 0.2–0.9 band Function's text suggests. Not measured in May.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/progesterone.md)

## Prolactin — in range (above Function's baseline)

**Measures:** a pituitary hormone best known for milk production that also affects hormones, metabolism, mood, and immunity in men.

**Why it matters:** high prolactin can suppress testosterone production and disturb LH and FSH, so it is checked in sexual dysfunction, infertility, and unexplained fatigue. It interacts with dopamine, which links it to mood and motivation.

**High / low:** high prolactin can reflect a benign pituitary tumor, hypothyroidism, or drugs such as antipsychotics, with low libido, erectile problems, infertility, mood change, or breast tissue growth. Low prolactin is less common and can accompany metabolic, autoimmune, or pituitary problems. The usual range is 2–18 ng/mL; Function names about 5 ng/mL a healthy baseline and defines no universal optimal. Stress, exercise, and some foods shift it through the day.

**Your result:** 9.9 ng/mL (May 19) → 14.9 ng/mL (Oct 2), in Quest range (2–18) and above Function's 5 ng/mL baseline; up 51% between draws.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/prolactin.md)

## Prostate Specific Antigen (PSA) %, Free — in range

**Measures:** the share of PSA in the blood that is free (unbound), as free PSA divided by total PSA.

**Why it matters:** when total PSA is 4–10 ng/mL, a low percent-free value raises the probability of cancer, while a high value usually reflects a benign cause such as enlargement or inflammation. Function's bands: above 25% lower probability; 10–25% intermediate, with MRI and urology referral suggested; below 10% higher probability. It also mentions a Function MRI that can look at the prostate.

**High / low:** the optimal result is above 25%. Age, inflammation, enlargement, drugs such as finasteride and corticosteroids, recent prostate procedures, vigorous cycling or heavy lifting, and ejaculation within 48 hours can shift the ratio. Function advises avoiding those for two days before testing.

**Your result:** 40% (calculated) on May 19 and Oct 2, in range. Quest > 25.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/prostate-specific-antigen-psa-percent-free.md)

## Prostate Specific Antigen (PSA), Free — in range

**Measures:** the unbound portion of PSA in the blood.

**Why it matters:** free PSA has no standalone range; clinicians use it only with total PSA to calculate percent-free PSA and sort benign causes from higher-risk ones when total PSA is 4–10 ng/mL. Function's summary for an in-range result says healthy prostate levels go with healthy weight, exercise, gut health, and low stress.

**Your result:** 0.2 ng/mL (May 19) → 0.2 ng/mL (Oct 2), in range. No Quest range applies.

## Prostate Specific Antigen (PSA), Total — in range

**Measures:** all PSA in the blood, free and protein-bound.

**Why it matters:** PSA leaks into blood from the prostate and can rise with inflammation, enlargement, or cancer, so trends over time matter more than one value. Function notes PSA screening can lead to overdiagnosis of slow-growing cancers and compares a high value to a neighborhood alarm that cannot say whether the cause is serious. It reads total PSA beside percent-free PSA, age, family history, and exam findings.

**High / low:** a low value usually means a healthy prostate, though obesity or certain drugs can lower PSA and treatment lowers it sharply. A value above 4.0 ng/mL (age and prostate size matter) may reflect prostatitis, enlargement, or cancer. Age-based ceilings: 40–49 ≤ 2.5; 50–59 ≤ 3.5; 60–69 ≤ 4.5; 70–79 ≤ 6.5; 80+ ≤ 7.2 ng/mL. Cycling, heavy lifting, and ejaculation within 48 hours can raise it.

**Your result:** 0.5 ng/mL (May 19) → 0.5 ng/mL (Oct 2), in range. Quest 0–4.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/prostate-specific-antigen-psa-total.md)

## Sex Hormone Binding Globulin (SHBG) — in range

**Measures:** a liver-made protein that binds sex hormones, mainly DHT, testosterone, and estradiol, and controls how much reaches tissues.

**Why it matters:** hormones cannot enter cells while bound, so SHBG sets how much testosterone is available. Function states SHBG moves inversely with insulin: higher insulin, as in insulin resistance, goes with lower SHBG and so more active hormone. Inflammation raises it. Age, weight, liver health, hyperthyroidism, and hormone therapy change it.

**High / low:** low SHBG means more free hormone activity, a point Function raises for low testosterone, libido, and acne or hair loss from DHT. High SHBG binds more testosterone and cuts the free fraction even when total looks normal. Function's range is 10–50 nmol/L for ages 18–55 and treats the lab range as optimal. Obesity associates with lower SHBG.

**Your result:** 13 nmol/L (May 19) → 15 nmol/L (Oct 2), in range at the low end. Quest and Function optimal 10–50. Fasting insulin over the same interval rose from 10.9 to 12.5 uIU/mL, the direction Function's inverse relationship predicts. The vault's earlier "SHBG sequesters Free T" reading does not hold at this level; see [[02-Indexes/topic_maps/health-performance-crossmap]].

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/sex-hormone-binding-globulin-shbg.md)

## Testosterone, Free — in range at Quest (far below Function optimal)

**Measures:** the unbound, biologically active fraction of testosterone, about 1–4% of the total.

**Why it matters:** free testosterone reflects what cells can use and can show a deficit when total looks normal, which makes it useful for diagnosing hypogonadism and guiding replacement therapy. It works with LH, SHBG, and estradiol. Function notes natural decline starts around age 35, and low testosterone associates with fatigue, depression, lost muscle, low libido, cardiovascular and metabolic risk. High values mostly follow anabolic steroids, testosterone therapy, or tumors and can bring polycythemia.

**High / low:** Function argues lab ranges reflect what is common, not what is healthy, and offers benchmarks from healthy men: under 40, 148.8–228.1 pg/mL; 40–59, 126.2–176.3; 60 and older, 97.3–131.8. Levels peak in the morning, fall about 1% a year after 30, and depend on deep sleep. When symptoms persist despite a normal result, Function points to testing for insulin resistance, gut health, and inflammation.

**Your result:** 54.0 pg/mL (May 19) → 64.1 pg/mL (Oct 2), in Quest range (35–155) and 57% below the bottom of Function's under-40 band (148.8). The protocol action trigger (< 150) is breached on both draws; see [[L1-Health/blood-analysis]].

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/testosterone-free.md)

## Testosterone, Total — in range at Quest (below Function optimal)

**Measures:** all testosterone in the blood: free plus albumin-bound plus SHBG-bound.

**Why it matters:** with SHBG, LH, and estradiol it helps tell whether fatigue, low libido, or mood change come from hormones, and it informs nutrition, lifestyle, or hormone therapy choices. Low values associate with weak muscle, depression, low libido, and higher cardiovascular and metabolic risk. High values mostly come from anabolic steroids, therapy, or tumors.

**High / low:** Function treats 500–1,000 ng/dL as ideal for vitality, notes most labs call 300–1,000 normal, and says values under 500 are common in aging men but usually reflect diet, inactivity, and stress, not age alone. It advises checking free testosterone and SHBG together because they set active availability. Testosterone peaks in the morning, drifts down about 1% a year after 30, and depends on deep sleep.

**Your result:** 300 ng/dL (May 19) → 285 ng/dL (Oct 2), in Quest range (250–1100) and below Function's 500–1,000 optimal band on both draws.
