# L1 — Liver Enzymes: ALT and AST Flags (Oct 2026)

**Synthesis purpose:** ALT and AST are the two new out-of-range biomarkers on the Oct 2, 2026 Function Health panel, and no vault page tracked the liver before this one. This page sets the data beside Function's cause list, Gil's own context in the vault, and the panel markers that bear on each cause. It does not diagnose. A physician interprets liver enzyme elevations.

**Headline:** ALT rose from 31 to 94 U/L (2.04× the upper limit of 46) and AST from 21 to 50 U/L (1.25× the upper limit of 40) in 136 days. ALP, bilirubin, and albumin stayed in range. GGT stayed inside Quest's range but above Function's tighter optimal band.

**Ratios:** AST/ALT was 0.68 in May and 0.53 in Oct. Function's AST page states that a ratio above 2 often aligns with alcohol-related liver injury, and that a lower ratio points toward viral, fatty, or drug-induced hepatocellular damage.

**Not measured:** CK (muscle injury), PT/INR, hepatitis B/C serologies, tests for Wilson's disease and alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, and any liver imaging. Function's ALT page lists PT/INR and imaging among follow-up tests, and its associated-conditions list names viral hepatitis, Wilson's disease, and alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency.

**What Function says about timing:** its ALT page notes that ALT can change day to day with diet, alcohol, intense exercise, medications, and supplements, that mild or borderline elevations are often transient, and that up to 30% of elevated liver enzyme results normalize within three weeks. A value 2× the limit is not borderline, and repeated elevation is what the page treats as the signal for further evaluation. The app's banner suggests a re-test in 2 months. The ALT page text says repeat every 6 months (3–6 months in the summary), and the AST page says 4–6 weeks after starting therapy in chronic conditions or 3–6 months when stable.

**Reading of the pattern (not a diagnosis):** The panel data narrows little. Nothing in range argues strongly against a metabolic or training-related cause, and several markers (insulin, triglycerides, LDL-P, GGT) point the same direction as the metabolic-dysfunction pathway on Function's pages. The unknowns that matter most are medications and supplements, alcohol, recent training, and the missing CK. A physician needs those answers before choosing the next test.

## Function's Follow-Up Menu (for the physician conversation)

Function's ALT page lists these as possible next steps, with the choice depending on risk profile and a physician's judgment:

- **Blood:** AST, ALP, GGT, bilirubin, albumin (done), PT/INR, blood counts (done), insulin, glucose, HbA1c, and an advanced lipid panel (done), kidney function (done), lipase and amylase (done in May), thyroid function (May)
- **Imaging:** liver ultrasound, CT, MRI with liver-fat quantification, MRCP, FibroScan (transient elastography)
- **Medical interventions and procedures:** left to a physician; biopsy appears only as a later-stage option

Function's ALT page also lists generic liver-support supplements (milk thistle, NAC, curcumin, vitamin E, alpha-lipoic acid, selenium). Its page advises speaking with a healthcare provider before starting any supplement. This page does not recommend adding them: the cause is unidentified, and the vault's current stack and medications are unconfirmed.
