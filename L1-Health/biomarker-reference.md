## Notes on Lab Range Variation

Different platforms use different reference ranges, which creates apparent contradictions:

| Biomarker | Standard Lab (Function Health) | Medicine 3.0 Optimal | Spren Optimal | Action Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free Testosterone | 46–224 pg/mL (male) | 148.8–228.1 pg/mL | — | < 150 pg/mL |
| Total Testosterone | 250–1100 ng/dL | 400–600+ ng/dL | 500–1200 ng/dL | < 400 ng/dL |
| TSH | 0.5–4.15 | — | 1–2 | — |
| Cortisol | 4–22 µg/dL | — | 10–15 ug/dL | — |

**Critical distinction — Free vs. Total Testosterone:**
- Standard labs report Total Testosterone. Most of it (96–99%) is bound to SHBG or albumin and biologically inert.
- Free T is the bioactive fraction (~1–4% of total). It is the only form that crosses cell membranes to drive muscle protein synthesis, cognitive function, and energy.
- Gil's May 2026 data: Total T = 300 ng/dL (borderline low, improving) vs. Free T = 54.0 pg/mL (critically low, action trigger breached). These two numbers tell completely different stories.
- **Always request Free T fractionation, not just Total T**, when assessing hormonal health.

Spren uses **functional/optimal** ranges designed for performance, not just disease avoidance. Gil's results look more concerning under Spren's lens than standard labs. Use Medicine 3.0 / Spren values as the performance optimization target; clinical ranges as the safety floor.
