# L1 — Biomarker Digest: Electrolytes

**What this group shows:** the blood minerals and buffers that run nerve signals, muscle contraction, fluid balance, and acid-base balance: calcium, potassium, chloride, and carbon dioxide (a read on bicarbonate). Function files sodium under Kidney ([[L1-Health/biomarker-digest-kidney]]) and RBC magnesium under Nutrients ([[L1-Health/biomarker-digest-nutrients]]). All four are in range at both draws.

**How to read this page:** each entry paraphrases Function Health's explainer text from the raw file in its **Source** line. Results are as of Oct 2, 2026; [[L1-Health/function-health-registry]] stays the source of truth for values. All four entries are Tier 2.

---

## Calcium — in range

**Measures:** blood calcium, which supports bones and teeth, muscle contraction (including the heart), nerve signaling, and clotting. About 99% sits in bone.

**Why it matters:** calcium works with magnesium, phosphorus, parathyroid hormone, and vitamin D: vitamin D helps absorb it and parathyroid hormone pulls it from bone when blood levels drop. Function reads a low value as a possible sign of kidney, thyroid, or vitamin D problems, not just low intake, and says to fix the cause before adding calcium.

**High / low:** high calcium (hypercalcemia) can mean overactive parathyroid glands, certain cancers, or too much vitamin D, with fatigue, thirst, kidney stones, and confusion. Low calcium (hypocalcemia) links to vitamin D deficiency, kidney problems, or underactive parathyroids, with cramps, tingling, and rarely seizures. Function's optimal range follows the lab's, 8.6–10.3 mg/dL. Diet, vitamin D, parathyroid hormone, cortisol, diuretics, and lithium move it, and it runs slightly higher in the early morning.

**Your result:** 9.6 mg/dL (May 19) → 9.1 mg/dL (Oct 2), in range. Quest and Function optimal 8.6–10.3. Function also lists calcium under Kidney and Nutrients.

## Carbon Dioxide — in range

**Measures:** total CO₂ in blood, an indirect read of bicarbonate, a main buffer for acid-base balance.

**Why it matters:** acid-base balance sets enzyme activity, oxygen delivery, and metabolism, and small bicarbonate shifts affect kidneys, brain, and muscle. The test can catch early imbalances and track treatment.

**High / low:** low CO₂ suggests metabolic acidosis from kidney problems, certain drugs (salicylates), shock, or hydration, blood sugar, nutrition, or breathing issues, with fast breathing, tiredness, confusion, nausea, or headache. High CO₂ suggests metabolic alkalosis from adrenal problems, long-term vomiting, or diuretics, with cramps, tingling, lightheadedness, or weakness. Function's page gives 20–32 mmol/L. Vomiting, diuretics, diarrhea, uncontrolled diabetes, and kidney disease shift it.

**Your result:** 26 mmol/L (May 19) → 28 mmol/L (Oct 2), in range. Quest 20–32; Function export optimal 23–32.

## Chloride — in range

**Measures:** the electrolyte that travels with sodium and helps regulate fluid, blood pressure, and acid-base balance; most of it comes from salt.

**Why it matters:** chloride keeps cells hydrated and helps nerves and muscles work. It usually moves with sodium and bicarbonate on the basic metabolic panel, and abnormal values can point to dehydration, kidney trouble, or lung disease. Diuretics change it.

**High / low:** low chloride (hypochloremia) follows prolonged vomiting, chronic diarrhea, diuretics, or adrenal disorders and often comes with low sodium or high bicarbonate (metabolic alkalosis). High chloride (hyperchloremia) follows kidney dysfunction, heavy salt intake, dehydration, or saline IV fluids and often comes with low bicarbonate (metabolic acidosis). Function's range is 98–110 mEq/L. Sweating, diuretics, digestive illness, fasting, and salty meals move it.

**Your result:** 104 mmol/L (May 19) → 103 mmol/L (Oct 2), in range. Quest and Function optimal 98–110.

## Potassium — in range

**Measures:** the electrolyte that regulates nerve signals, muscle contraction (especially the heart), and fluid balance. The kidneys keep it in range.

**Why it matters:** potassium works with sodium, chloride, and bicarbonate for cell function, blood pressure, and acid-base balance. When the kidneys fail it can climb to unsafe levels, so Function reads it with creatinine and BUN, and it shows how diuretics, steroids, and other medications act on electrolytes. Poor regulation raises the risk of heart rhythm and muscle problems.

**High / low:** high potassium (hyperkalemia) can cause an irregular heartbeat, muscle weakness, and heavy limbs and often follows kidney disease, dehydration, ACE inhibitors, or potassium-sparing diuretics. Low potassium (hypokalemia) can cause fatigue, cramps, and a fluttery heartbeat after vomiting, diarrhea, sweating, or diuretics. Diet, hydration, sweat, medications, kidney and adrenal function, and stress hormones move it. Function suggests rechecking every 6–12 months or after medication changes.

**Your result:** 3.9 mmol/L (May 19) → 4.0 mmol/L (Oct 2), in range. Quest and Function optimal 3.5–5.3.
