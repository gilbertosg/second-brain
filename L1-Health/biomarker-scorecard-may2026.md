## Critical New Findings

### Finding 1: Free Testosterone — 54.0 pg/mL (FIRST MEASUREMENT)

**This is the highest-priority new discovery in this ingest.**

The vault tracked Total Testosterone (262 ng/dL → 300 ng/dL) but never had a Free Testosterone measurement. Free testosterone is the biologically active fraction — the testosterone that actually enters cells, stimulates the androgen receptor, and drives muscle protein synthesis, dopamine production, cognitive function, and libido.

| Metric | Value | Protocol Optimal | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free Testosterone | 54.0 pg/mL | 148.8–228.1 pg/mL (under 40) | −94.8 pg/mL below minimum optimal |
| Free T as % of optimal minimum | 36% | 100% | −64% |
| Action trigger | < 150 pg/mL | — | **BREACHED** |

At 54.0 pg/mL, Gil has 36% of the minimum Medicine 3.0 optimal free testosterone. This explains why symptoms of low testosterone (fatigue, cognitive fog, reduced motivation) persist even though Total T at 300 ng/dL sits at the low end of "normal" on standard lab ranges.

**Why Free T can be low when Total T is borderline-normal:**
Sex Hormone Binding Globulin (SHBG) binds testosterone, making it biologically unavailable. High SHBG → low Free T despite adequate Total T. At Gil's previous measurement (Jan 2024), SHBG was 12 nmol/L (normal range). However, conditions that raise SHBG — chronic stress, low caloric intake, insulin resistance, elevated cortisol — are all present in Gil's profile. A retest of SHBG is warranted.

**Protocol action (from Gil-Health-Upgrade-Protocol-Proposal.md):**
If Free T remains below 150 pg/mL across two consecutive quarterly panels, clinical consultation is warranted for Clomiphene Citrate, Enclomiphene, or direct TRT. One panel confirmed — the next quarterly test will determine whether pharmacological intervention is mathematically warranted.
