## Antinuclear Antibodies (ANA) Screen — negative (May only)

**Measures:** antibodies that mistakenly target the cell's own nuclear material.

**Why it matters:** ANA screens for autoimmune disease such as lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, and Sjögren's syndrome, and it is rarely read alone. A positive result leads to specific antibody tests (anti-dsDNA) and inflammation markers (hs-CRP, ESR). Function also treats a positive ANA as a prompt to look at triggers: infections, stress, environmental exposures, and gut health.

**High / low:** a negative ANA is reassuring and optimal, though some autoimmune conditions can still exist, particularly early or when disease affects one area. A positive result links more strongly to lupus or Sjögren's, especially with joint pain, fatigue, rashes, or dry eyes and mouth. Function advises sharing results and a symptom diary with a clinician when symptoms persist or hs-CRP stays elevated.

**Your result:** negative (May 19; not retested). Quest and Function optimal: negative.

## Rheumatoid Factor (RF) — in range (May only)

**Measures:** antibodies that attack healthy joints, glands, or other normal cells.

**Why it matters:** rheumatoid factor links to rheumatoid arthritis, rheumatoid vasculitis, and Sjögren's syndrome. Function notes healthy people can carry higher levels, which it calls pre-autoimmunity, and says triggers such as dairy, gluten, corn, heavy metals, and chronic stress are worth identifying.

**High / low:** below 14 IU/mL is the normal (negative) range and most often means no rheumatoid arthritis or autoimmune Sjögren's, although some people with those conditions still have a low or negative RF. A value of 14 or above counts as positive.

**Your result:** < 10 IU/mL (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest < 14; Function optimal 0–14.
