## Arachidonic Acid/EPA Ratio — in range (May only)

**Measures:** the balance between arachidonic acid (AA, a pro-inflammatory omega-6) and EPA (an anti-inflammatory omega-3).

**Why it matters:** a Western diet (red and processed meat, refined seed oils, little fish) pushes the ratio high and leans the body toward inflammation. Function reads it with CRP and IL-6, links a high ratio to heart disease, metabolic syndrome, and diabetes, and uses it to track dietary change.

**High / low:** a high ratio goes with chronic inflammation and cardiovascular and metabolic risk; an in-range ratio reflects a more anti-inflammatory state, though extremely low values could impair immune and healing responses.

**Your result:** 20.2 (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest and Function optimal 3.7–40.7.

## Ferritin — in range (May only)

**Measures:** the protein that stores iron, a window into iron reserves.

**Why it matters:** ferritin often shifts before serum iron does, so it catches deficiency and overload early (including hemochromatosis). It is also an acute-phase reactant, rising with inflammation, infection, or liver disease, so Function reads it with CRP to separate iron problems from inflammation-driven spikes.

**High / low:** low ferritin means low iron stores, with fatigue, poor concentration, and weaker immunity, and can signal poor absorption or chronic blood loss. High ferritin can mean iron overload, ongoing inflammation, liver disease, infection, or metabolic problems such as obesity and insulin resistance. Function treats above 30 ng/mL as enough to avoid deficiency, and says that when inflammation is present clinicians aim higher, about 100–300 ng/mL for men; its exported optimal band here is 70–150. Illness, intense exercise, iron-rich meals, fasting, and blood donation move it.

**Your result:** 98 ng/mL (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest 38–380; Function optimal 70–150. hs-CRP was 1.2 mg/L, above range, on the same day.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/ferritin.md)

## Homocysteine — in range at Quest (above Function optimal)

**Measures:** an amino acid formed when the body breaks down methionine; clearing it takes vitamin B12, folate, and B6.

**Why it matters:** a shortfall in those nutrients or a methylation gene variant lets homocysteine build up, which can damage blood vessel linings and raises the risk of heart disease, stroke, cognitive decline, and weaker bones; in pregnancy it raises the risk of complications. Function reads it with serum B12, folate, B6, and hs-CRP.

**High / low:** high homocysteine can reflect B-vitamin deficiency, methylation variants, kidney disease, smoking, or alcohol. Very low values (under 4 umol/L) can limit glutathione production. Function's male bands: optimal 6.0–8.0; slightly above 8.0–12.0; above 12.0–20.0; above 20 needs medical attention. Diet, stress, hydration, activity, and genetics move it.

**Your result:** 10.0 umol/L (May 19; not retested), in Quest range (≤ 13.5) and in Function's "slightly above" band (8.0–12.0). The vault logged 7.55 in Jan 2026 (Spren, different range).

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/homocysteine.md)

## Iron — in range (May only)

**Measures:** the iron circulating in serum, bound to the carrier transferrin.

**Why it matters:** iron is part of hemoglobin and drives oxygen transport, energy, and DNA synthesis. Function reads it with ferritin, transferrin saturation, and TIBC. Low serum iron tracks with low ferritin in iron deficiency anemia; high iron and saturation can signal hemochromatosis.

**High / low:** low iron comes from poor intake, blood loss, or poor absorption (celiac disease, inflammation) and brings fatigue and dizziness. High iron comes from hemochromatosis, over-supplementation, or chronic liver disease and can deposit in liver, heart, and pancreas. Levels swing 30–50% across the day, so Function advises a fasting morning draw; infections and stress lower it through hepcidin.

**Your result:** 89 mcg/dL (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest and Function optimal 50–180.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/iron.md)

## Iron % Saturation — in range (May only)

**Measures:** the share of transferrin carrying iron (serum iron divided by TIBC, times 100).

**Why it matters:** it shows how efficiently iron is transported and used. High saturation with high ferritin suggests overload; low saturation with low ferritin suggests deficiency. It complements hemoglobin and hematocrit when typing anemia.

**High / low:** low saturation suggests too little available iron, from poor absorption or chronic inflammation. High saturation signals excess iron that can cause oxidative stress and liver, heart, or joint damage. Morning fasting draws give the most reliable value.

**Your result:** 29% (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest and Function optimal 20–48.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/iron-percent-saturation.md)

## Iron Binding Capacity — in range (May only)

**Measures:** total iron-binding capacity (TIBC): how many transferrin "seats" the blood has available to carry iron.

**Why it matters:** read with serum iron, saturation, and ferritin, it separates iron deficiency from overload.

**High / low:** high TIBC usually means the body lacks iron, so more transferrin sits empty. Low TIBC can mean excess iron or a condition such as liver disease or inflammation affecting iron handling. A fasting morning draw avoids day-to-day swings.

**Your result:** 305 mcg/dL (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest 250–425; Function optimal 250–450.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/iron-binding-capacity.md)

## Magnesium, RBC — in range (May only)

**Measures:** magnesium inside red blood cells, a closer read of cellular stores than serum magnesium.

**Why it matters:** magnesium runs more than 300 enzyme systems, steadies heart rhythm, relaxes muscle, balances calcium and potassium, and helps activate vitamin D. About 99% sits inside cells or bone, so serum levels can look normal while stores run low. Function notes that correcting a deficiency may help migraines and persistent hypertension.

**High / low:** low magnesium links to muscle twitches, insomnia, heightened stress responses, and over time type 2 diabetes and osteoporosis, and highly processed food, chronic stress, and diuretics deplete it. High magnesium is rare and usually reflects kidney impairment or heavy supplementation. Stress, hard exercise, poor sleep, dehydration, and blood sugar swings shift it.

**Your result:** 6.4 mg/dL (May 19; not retested), in range at the top of the band. Quest 4–6.4; Function optimal 5–6.4.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/magnesium-rbc.md)

## Methylmalonic Acid (MMA) — in range (May only)

**Measures:** an acid that builds up when vitamin B12 is too scarce to convert it for energy production.

**Why it matters:** MMA is a sensitive B12 marker that can reveal a hidden deficiency even when serum B12 looks normal. Function reads it with serum B12 and homocysteine.

**High / low:** high MMA typically signals B12 deficiency (with neurological symptoms, anemia, and poor energy metabolism) and can also reflect kidney or metabolic problems. Normal or low MMA means B12 status and the related metabolism work. Function's bands: optimal 87–250 nmol/L; above 250–350; above 350 needs attention.

**Your result:** 91 nmol/L (May 19; not retested), in range at the low end. Quest 55–335; Function optimal 87–250.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/methylmalonic-acid-mma.md)

## Omega 6 / 3 Ratio — in range at Quest (above Function optimal)

**Measures:** the ratio of omega-6 to omega-3 fatty acids in the blood.

**Why it matters:** it snapshots inflammatory balance. Typical Western diets run 10:1 to 20:1, which pushes lab ranges wide. Research Function cites links a ratio near 2:1 to 4:1 to less chronic inflammation, lower heart-disease risk, better brain function and mood, and healthier metabolism and immune regulation. It complements hs-CRP, IL-6, glucose, and triglycerides and shows the dietary causes upstream.

**High / low:** Function's optimal ratio is 2.0–4.0. A ratio below the lab's normal is a good sign; above 4.0 leaves room to improve.

**Your result:** 8.3 (May 19; not retested), in Quest range (3.7–14.4) and above Function's optimal band (2–4).

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/omega-6-3-ratio.md)

## Omega-3 Total / OmegaCheck — 🔴 below range (May only)

**Measures:** EPA, DHA, and DPA together as a percentage of all phospholipid fatty acids in the blood.

**Why it matters:** the body cannot make enough of these fats, so the number shows how well food and supplements keep it stocked, more reliably than diet tracking. They build cells, regulate inflammation, and support heart and brain. Function notes the test is used for people with a family history of heart disease, high hs-CRP, or omega-3 supplementation, and that it is not comparable with red-cell omega-3 indexes, which use a different method and scale.

**High / low:** an optimal result is 5.5% by weight or higher, linked to lower risk of cardiovascular disease, heart attack, and stroke. A below-range result means low intake of fatty fish or an overload of omega-6 from processed food and seed oils, and low omega-3 impairs inflammation control with possible links to cardiovascular disease, cognitive decline, and mood disorders.

**Your result:** **5.0% by weight** (May 19; not retested), below range. Quest ≥ 5.5; Function optimal > 5.4. The vault's protocol stack includes omega-3 at 2 g/day; Function's retest guidance is 8–12 weeks after starting a plan, so an Oct retest would have fit.

**Causes (Function):** too little fatty fish or algae; high omega-6 intake; poor conversion of plant ALA to EPA; stress and a sedentary life; poor gut health; insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome; chronic inflammation (which raises omega-3 demand); oxidative stress; FADS1 and FADS2 gene variants; aging. Statins (atorvastatin is named) can lower circulating omega-3, and alcohol and smoking also contribute.

**Follow-up (Function):** physician-guided plan; medications it lists for related conditions are NSAIDs and anti-diabetic drugs such as metformin; retest 8–12 weeks after starting dietary, supplement, or lifestyle changes, more often with fat-absorption or inflammation problems.

**Levers Function lists** *(its text, for physician discussion)*: eat fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel, anchovies) two to three times a week, or algal oil if avoiding fish; limit trans fats, ultra-processed foods heavy in seed oils, and large amounts of red and processed meat; moderate aerobic activity, resistance training, balanced weight, and good sleep. Supplements it names: EPA and DHA at 500–1,000 mg combined daily (about 600 mg EPA to 400 mg DHA per 1,000 mg), algal oil (200–500 mg), flaxseed oil (1,000–2,000 mg), krill oil (500–1,000 mg). Function advises a clinician review before any supplement.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/omega-3-total-omegacheck.md)

## Omega-3: DHA — in range (May only)

**Measures:** docosahexaenoic acid, the omega-3 that structures brain, retina, and cell membranes.

**Why it matters:** DHA supports cognition, vision, and heart health, and demand rises with inflammation, illness, or stress. It must come from fatty fish, algae, or supplements. Function reads it with EPA and the omega-6 to omega-3 ratio.

**High / low:** low DHA comes from too little omega-3 intake, poor ALA conversion, or omega-6 excess and may bring cognitive difficulty, cardiovascular risk, and inflammation. High DHA is rare and usually reflects heavy supplementation, with a possible bleeding risk.

**Your result:** 3.0% by weight (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest and Function optimal 1.4–5.1.

## Omega-3: DPA — in range (May only)

**Measures:** docosapentaenoic acid, the intermediate between EPA and DHA that also reserves either form.

**Why it matters:** DPA reduces inflammation and supports brain, heart, and vascular function. The body makes little from plant ALA, so fish and marine sources matter. Function reads a low DPA, or a high omega-6 to omega-3 ratio, as a sign of chronic inflammation.

**High / low:** low DPA suggests low omega-3 intake or high inflammatory demand. High DPA usually reflects an omega-3-rich diet or supplements and is not a concern.

**Your result:** 1.4% by weight (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest and Function optimal 0.8–1.8.

## Omega-3: EPA — in range (May only)

**Why it matters:** EPA produces molecules that calm the immune response and support healing. The body converts almost none from plant ALA, so it depends on fatty fish, algae, or supplements. A higher EPA share relative to omega-6 goes with an anti-inflammatory state.

## Omega-6: Arachidonic Acid — in range (May only)

**Measures:** the omega-6 fat in cell membranes that the body turns into either inflammatory or resolving signals.

**Why it matters:** enzymes convert AA into prostaglandins and leukotrienes, which drive inflammation after injury, or into lipoxins, which resolve it. Omega-3 EPA and DHA balance its inflammatory side, so Function reads it with them and with the AA/EPA ratio.

**High / low:** high AA associates with chronic inflammation, cardiovascular disease, metabolic syndrome, and autoimmune disorders. Low AA can impair healing and immune response.

**Your result:** 13.7% by weight (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest and Function optimal 8.6–15.6.

## Omega-6: Linoleic Acid — in range (May only)

**Measures:** the essential omega-6 fat found in vegetable oils, nuts, seeds, meat, and eggs.

**Why it matters:** the body needs LA for cell membranes and skin ceramides, but modern diets overshoot. Excess LA converts to AA, oxidizes into compounds (OXLAMs) that add oxidative stress, and chronically high levels link to heart disease and Alzheimer's disease.

**High / low:** high LA reflects a diet heavy in processed and refined oils and can feed inflammation, oxidative stress, and metabolic syndrome. Low LA can weaken the skin barrier and may affect immune function.

## Vitamin D — in range

**Measures:** blood vitamin D, a nutrient that acts like a hormone.

**Why it matters:** vitamin D drives calcium absorption and bone strength, and through its receptor regulates more than 900 genes for immunity, cell growth, hormone signaling, and detoxification. When it is low, parathyroid hormone rises, which can weaken bone over time, and low vitamin D can increase inflammation and affect sex hormones, thyroid function, and insulin sensitivity. The skin makes it from sunlight; limited sun, indoor life, and darker skin lower production.

**High / low:** low vitamin D means poor calcium absorption, weaker bone, osteoporosis risk, and reduced defense against infection. High levels are uncommon and follow over-supplementation, with calcium buildup that can cause nausea, kidney problems, or confusion. Function's optimal range is 45–100 ng/mL; below 45 is "below" and above 100 is "above."

**Your result:** 54 ng/mL (May 19) → 52 ng/mL (Oct 2), in range. Quest 30–100; Function optimal 45–100.

**Causes (Function):** causes it lists for low vitamin D are advanced age, certain medications, intestinal malabsorption (ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease, celiac disease), lack of sun exposure, osteoporosis, and weight-loss surgery.

**Source:** [raw file](../00-Raw_Sources/L1_Health/function-health/2026-10-02/vitamin-d.md)

## Zinc — in range (May only)

**Measures:** serum zinc, a mineral for immunity, wound healing, cell growth, and carbohydrate metabolism.

**Why it matters:** zinc activates hundreds of enzymes, enhances insulin action, supports DNA synthesis, and sustains taste and smell. It balances against copper like a seesaw. Low zinc with high hs-CRP can mean inflammation is pulling zinc out of the blood into tissues.

**High / low:** low zinc links to frequent infections, slow healing, loss of taste or smell, gut disorders, and chronic stress. High zinc is rare and follows heavy supplementation or industrial exposure, with nausea or copper deficiency. Function's optimal range matches the lab's, 60–130 mcg/dL. Levels run higher in the morning, fall with inflammation or illness, and shift after meals.

**Your result:** 75 mcg/dL (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest and Function optimal 60–130.
