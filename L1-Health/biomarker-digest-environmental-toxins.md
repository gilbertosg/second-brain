## Lead — in range at Quest (Function target is zero)

**Measures:** lead in blood, which reflects recent exposure rather than the full body burden stored in bone.

**Why it matters:** lead accumulates in bones, teeth, kidneys, liver, and brain, and about 95% sits in bone and teeth. Exposure comes from homes built before 1978 with lead paint, contaminated soil and dust, old pipes, certain jobs (renovation, auto repair), imported goods, and some folk remedies. It can sit silently for years, so testing finds exposure early. Function reads it with kidney panels and hs-CRP.

**High / low:** low lead reflects minimal exposure and effective elimination. Elevated lead can cause oxidative stress, neurotoxicity, cognitive and behavioral problems, cardiovascular strain, impaired kidney function, and fertility problems. Function says no amount is safe and defines no optimal level, so the lab's reference limit guides action. Lead stored in bone re-enters blood during stress, pregnancy, or illness.

**Your result:** 1.0 mcg/dL (May 19; not retested), in range. Quest < 3.5; Function export shows a zero target.

## Mercury — in range at Quest (Function target is zero)

**Measures:** mercury in blood, which mostly reflects recent exposure.

**Why it matters:** exposure comes from seafood (large fish such as tuna and swordfish carry methylmercury), dental amalgam fillings, and industrial pollution. Elemental mercury vapor, inorganic mercury, and organic methylmercury affect the body differently, with methylmercury most likely to reach the brain and nervous system. Function reads mercury with creatinine, glutathione, selenium, and zinc as signs of detox capacity.

**High / low:** elevated mercury can lead to memory loss, trouble concentrating, mood shifts, numbness, tremors, kidney stress, and oxidative stress. Function says the optimal level is zero and sorts results by severity: 1–5 slightly elevated, 5–10 moderately elevated, above 10 high, even when a result sits inside the lab's limit. Blood levels spike after high-mercury fish and clear over weeks to months for methylmercury, longer for elemental mercury.

**Your result:** < 4 mcg/L (May 19; not retested), in range at Quest (≤ 10). The result is reported only as under 4, so it cannot show whether it falls in Function's 1–5 "slightly elevated" band.
