## Why it matters

Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) is an omega-3 fatty acid that is essential for brain health, cardiovascular function, and inflammatory balance. It is a major structural component of the brain, retina, and cell membranes, helping to support cognitive function, vision, and cellular health. Because the body has limited ability to produce DHA from other fats, it must come from dietary sources like fatty fish, algae oil, or supplements. This makes it especially important during times of high inflammation, chronic illness, or stress when the demand for omega-3s increases. DHA is particularly important for brain development and health, helping to lower the risk of conditions like depression, ADHD, dementia, and Alzheimer’s disease.

DHA works closely with other omega-3 fatty acids, such as EPA, to regulate inflammation and counterbalance the pro-inflammatory effects of omega-6 fats like arachidonic acid. Measuring DHA alongside EPA and the omega-6 to omega-3 ratio provides a clear picture of inflammatory health and cardiovascular risk. This biomarker also offers insights into the effectiveness of omega-3 supplementation and diet in supporting brain and heart health.

Abnormal DHA levels can have distinct implications:

- **Low levels** : Often linked to insufficient omega-3 intake, poor conversion of alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), a plant-based omega-3, or an imbalance with omega-6 fats reflecting increased inflammation. This may lead to cognitive challenges, increased cardiovascular risk, or heightened inflammation.
- **High levels** : Typically due to excessive supplementation. While rare, elevated levels may require balancing with other fatty acids to avoid potential bleeding risks or fatty acid imbalances.

Monitoring DHA levels helps identify whether dietary changes or supplements are needed to support optimal brain, heart, and inflammatory health. Ensuring a proper balance of omega-3 and omega-6 fats is key to maintaining these essential systems.

## Summary

An in-range level of DHA shows the body has enough of this essential fat to support brain function, vision, and a healthy inflammatory response. It reflects stable structural support for cell membranes in the brain and eyes, and a lower risk of conditions like cognitive decline, macular degeneration, and neuroinflammation. When DHA is in the optimal range, symptoms are generally absent, but it plays a quiet, steady role in memory, focus, mood stability, and maintaining the integrity of the nervous system and retinal cells. Staying in range helps protect long-term brain and eye health and supports resilience against age-related cognitive and visual decline.

To maintain DHA in range, a root cause approach focuses on regular intake of cold-water fatty fish or algae-based DHA supplements, particularly for those with limited seafood in their diet. Antioxidants like vitamin E or alpha-lipoic acid help protect DHA from oxidation and preserve its activity in cell membranes. Botanicals like curcumin may further enhance DHA’s anti-inflammatory benefits. Maintaining a healthy gut microbiome through probiotics and prebiotic-rich foods also supports nutrient absorption. Ongoing omega-3 testing allows for personalized adjustments to keep DHA in range, helping protect brain and visual health over time.
