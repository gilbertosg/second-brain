# Glucose - Urine

Elevated fasting glucose levels in urine can be caused by type 1 or 2 diabetes, kidney, liver, or hormonal issues.

## Why it matters

When healthy, glucose levels in urine are generally too low to be detected.

However, after very high-sugar meals (such as Thanksgiving dinner), it is not uncommon to temporarily see glucose in urine. If you properly fasted and your test detects glucose, closely review your blood glucose and HbA1c tests. However, most of the time, glucose appears in the urine, it is due to uncontrolled type 1 or type 2 diabetes.

## Summary

It is important to understand the root cause of glucose in the urine.

## Causes

Although there was no glucose in your urine at the time of your test, it’s important to understand what may cause it to happen.

Glucose in urine, or glucosuria, can typically be caused by type 1 and type 2 diabetes (when blood sugars are chronically high). However, it can also show up with kidney problems that impact glucose absorption, liver abnormalities, or hormonal disorders. Glucose in the urine may also show up in women who are pregnant.

## Foods to eat

The below are beneficial in maintaining healthy glucose levels.

- Grass-fed, regeneratively, or pasture-raised meats, poultry, and fish (if accessible).
- Good fats, such as olive oil, avocados, nuts, and seeds.
- Vegetables (all except for starchy root vegetables, like potatoes, which are best eaten in moderation).
- High-fiber, prebiotic, and probiotic foods are helpful to the microbiome, an important system that helps regulate blood glucose.
- Certain foods and spices may have particular benefits for glucose control and insulin resistance, including bitter melon, cinnamon, fenugreek, ginger, and turmeric.

**Time-restricted eating is also helpful with metabolic control.**

- Avoid eating three hours before bed.
- Leave at least 12 hours—ideally 14 to 16 hours—between dinner and breakfast.

**Order of eating foods**

- Eat vegetables, fat, and protein before any carbohydrates.
- Start the day with protein and fat for breakfast. Try reducing or avoiding carbohydrates or sugar at breakfast.
- Avoid bread, alcohol, or any refined carbohydrate foods before eating vegetables, fat, or protein in a meal.

## Foods to limit

For glucose control, it is beneficial to dramatically reduce or eliminate all ultra-processed food, sugars in any form (including all liquid sugar calories in sugar-sweetened beverages), and refined starches.

## Self care

Daily exercise is essential for regulating blood sugar.

It is important to do a little exercise after eating, especially your biggest meal. This could be a 20-30-minute walk, which helps clear glucose from the blood and allows it to be utilized by the muscles.

More vigorous cardiovascular exercise and strength training are essential for glucose control and long-term health. Ideally, 30-60 minutes of vigorous walking, jogging, or equivalent five days a week. It’s also beneficial to incorporate 30 minutes of strength training three to four times a week.
