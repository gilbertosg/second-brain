## Why it matters

Blood urea nitrogen (BUN) is a waste product made in the liver when it breaks down protein into ammonia, which is then converted into urea. Urea travels through the bloodstream to the kidneys, where it’s filtered out and excreted in urine. If BUN levels are high, it may mean the kidneys aren’t filtering waste effectively. However, it can also indicate other factors like dehydration or high protein intake. BUN also gives insight into liver function and protein metabolism because the liver makes urea during the process of breaking down protein. If the liver isn’t working well, or if protein intake is very high or low, BUN levels can change.

Typically, this test is compared with creatinine to monitor the kidneys' ability to regulate waste. In the case of kidney disease, waste material can build up in the blood and may lead to serious health problems. In the absence of elevated creatinine, it suggests there may be excessive dietary protein intake, usually in the form of supplemental amino acids. Regular monitoring of BUN is essential for individuals with kidney disease or those undergoing treatments like dialysis, helping healthcare providers adjust treatment plans effectively.

- **High BUN:** May be a sign that the kidneys are not filtering waste effectively. It can also result from dehydration, a high-protein diet, or increased protein breakdown due to illness or internal bleeding.
- **Low BUN:** Low BUN may point to liver dysfunction or malnutrition. It can also be seen with a very low-protein diet or overhydration, which dilutes urea levels in the blood.

**Summary**

Measuring blood urea nitrogen (BUN) is used for evaluating kidney function and overall health. Elevated BUN levels can indicate impaired kidney function. Additionally, BUN levels help assess hydration status, as dehydration can cause an increase in BUN, while overhydration can lower it. This makes BUN a useful indicator for both kidney health and fluid balance.

**Optimal ranges**

Optimal ranges are often narrower and meant to identify patterns of a disease before it manifests. It is important to monitor changes in your results over time for a reliable understanding of your health.*

**What makes this biomarker fluctuate**

BUN can fluctuate depending on hydration status (dehydration can increase BUN), and the amount of protein in a diet: high protein intake or a recent high-protein meal may increase BUN, whereas low protein or starvation may decrease BUN.

## Summary

A normal blood urea nitrogen (BUN) level suggests that the liver is efficiently processing proteins and that the kidneys are properly filtering waste. A comprehensive approach to health assessment should consider BUN results alongside other markers of kidney function, liver health, and overall metabolic status. This comprehensive perspective allows for a more accurate interpretation of BUN levels and can help identify potential imbalances or areas for optimization even when results fall within the normal range.

## Foods to eat

You can help support healthy creatinine levels with proper hydration and healthy amounts of protein in your diet.
