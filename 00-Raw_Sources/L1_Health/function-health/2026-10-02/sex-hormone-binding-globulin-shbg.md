## Why it matters

Sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) is a protein primarily made in the liver. It binds tightly to the main sex hormones and guides them out of tissues and into the blood. Kind of like a bouncer at a club, SHBG patrols the number of sex hormones that your body tissues can use, which balances sex hormone-related processes, such as sperm production, libido, and testosterone availability in the body. Hormones can’t enter cells when they are bound to something else, so the rate of hormones getting into their target cells greatly depends on how much SHBG the liver produces. SHBGs prioritize androgens (male hormones) and bind to the following in order of affinity: dihydrotestosterone (DHT), testosterone, androstenediol, estradiol, then estrone.

SHBG is intended to balance out hormones so you don’t have too much or too little at one time. If you have a low SHBG it signifies your available sex hormones may have more activity on their target cells. Important to note: SHBG correlates inversely (the opposite) with the insulin hormone. Meaning, that higher levels of insulin—as seen in insulin resistance—correspond to lower levels of SHBG. So, the ON button for hormones listed above is high in insulin-resistant individuals. This is a critical point when thinking about the management of low testosterone, low sex drive, and androgen-related conditions like acne and hair loss due to elevated DHT.

SHBG increases when inflammation is present. It seems to be a way of protecting us as if the body is saying “let’s not prioritize reproduction right now.” Inflammation is measured by the lab test hs-CRP, although not always linked.

Test results may vary depending on your age, gender, health history, weight, the health of your liver, if you have hyperthyroidism (Graves’ Disease), and/or if you’re on hormone replacement therapy.

## Summary

A sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) level within the normal range suggests a healthy balance in the transport and regulation of sex hormones, such as testosterone and estradiol. Normal SHBG levels suggest that the body is effectively managing the distribution of free and bound testosterone, which is key for maintaining various physiological functions, including muscle mass, bone density, and libido. Maintaining SHBG within the normal range is key for processes like reproduction, energy regulation, and metabolic health.

Maintaining normal SHBG levels from a root cause perspective involves addressing several lifestyle factors. Regular physical activity and maintaining a healthy body weight are vital, as obesity is strongly associated with lower SHBG levels. A diet rich in whole foods like extra-virgin olive oil, omega-3 fatty acids, and micronutrients like magnesium supports liver health, where SHBG is primarily synthesized. By focusing on these fundamental aspects of health, the body can maintain optimal SHBG levels and overall hormonal balance.
