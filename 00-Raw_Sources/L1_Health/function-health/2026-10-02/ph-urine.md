## Why it matters

Urine is composed of metabolites and waste products the body must eliminate. Urine also contains nutrients, electrolytes, and water, which impact pH balance. The acidity of urine can change when the balance of those compounds changes, and a pH urine test reveals whether urine is acidic or alkaline.

**Low pH/Acidic:** Urine becomes more acidic when the body retains excess sodium or acid. Common factors include a diet high in fish, meat products, or cheese or one low in fruits and vegetables; diabetic ketoacidosis, a complication of diabetes; diarrhea or vomiting; or starvation.

**High pH/Alkaline:** When the body retains excess “base,” the urine becomes more basic or “alkaline.” Common factors include a diet high in fruits, vegetables, or non-dairy cheese or milk products; kidney stones; urinary tract infections (UTIs); or kidney-related disorders. Ideally, urine should be more alkaline than acidic.

The body’s natural pH is slightly alkaline at 7.5. However, any urine pH over 6.5 is considered normal.

The goal of testing pH is to identify whether or not you have an acid-base imbalance, to determine how severe the imbalance is, and to help diagnose underlying diseases or conditions (such as diabetic ketoacidosis, a life-threatening complication of diabetes).

Testing also helps monitor critical illnesses that affect acid-base balance, such as chronic lung disease and kidney disease.

## Summary

Your pH balance is healthy. To maintain this, eat a wide range of fruits and vegetables, and try not to over consume coffee or alcohol, if either are a regular practice for you. Both of these can adversely impact pH.

## Causes

pH levels fluctuate for many reasons, but diet and hydration play a role, as well (as mentioned above).

Sometimes pH will lower due to decreased acid excretion through the kidneys, increased acid through foods, or loss of bicarbonate. High urine pH can be caused by kidney stones, kidney tubular acidosis, urinary tract infection (UTI), or vomiting or stomach pumping (gastric suction).

## Self care

The following can help maintain pH levels:

-Staying hydrated, which includes drinking water throughout the day.

-Maintaining a diet rich in whole foods, vegetables, and fruits. Try to reduce ultra-processed foods, if possible, and refined sugars and starches.

-Visiting your provider on a regular basis to closely monitor treatments for other conditions, including heart, liver, and kidney disease.
