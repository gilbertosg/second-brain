## Why it matters

Carbon dioxide (CO₂) gives an indirect look at the amount of bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) in the blood. Bicarbonate is one of the body’s main tools for keeping the acid-base balance in check, which is critical for enzyme activity, getting oxygen to tissues, and keeping metabolism running smoothly. Even small changes in bicarbonate levels can affect how key organs like the kidneys, brain, and muscles function.

If CO₂ levels are too low, it may mean the body is in a state called metabolic acidosis. This can happen when the kidneys aren’t working properly, with certain medications (such as salicylates), during shock, or when there are issues with hydration, blood sugar, nutrition, or breathing. Signs of metabolic acidosis might include fast breathing, tiredness, confusion, nausea, or headaches.

If CO₂ levels are too high, it might point to metabolic alkalosis. This can be caused by problems with adrenal glands, losing too much stomach acid from long-term vomiting, or fluid imbalances from diuretics or other conditions. Symptoms can include muscle cramps, tingling, feeling lightheaded, or weakness.

Since bicarbonate is influenced by many systems in the body, the CO₂ test is a useful tool for catching early acid-base imbalances, tracking how treatment is going, and helping identify possible metabolic issues—sometimes even before symptoms show up.

**Optimal ranges**

The optimal range for carbon dioxide is 20-32 mmol/L.

**What makes this biomarker fluctuate**

This can fluctuate primarily in response to changes in metabolism, kidney function, hydration, and the body’s acid-base balance. Factors such as vomiting or diuretic use can increase bicarbonate levels by causing loss of stomach acid or shifting fluids, while conditions like diarrhea, uncontrolled diabetes, or kidney disease may lower bicarbonate by increasing acid production or impairing acid excretion. Because bicarbonate serves as a key buffer, these fluctuations can signal how the body is managing metabolic stresses and maintaining chemical balance. Understanding how CO₂ shifts helps doctors assess overall metabolic health and electrolyte balance.

## Summary

A carbon dioxide test result that falls within the normal range generally reflects a healthy balance between acid production and the body’s ability to respond to it. It suggests that the body’s metabolic processes are working well together to maintain a stable blood pH.

A normal carbon dioxide level is a positive sign that the body’s acid-base regulation system is intact, supporting cardiovascular health, brain function, muscle performance, and overall vitality. In most cases, an in-range carbon dioxide result will not cause symptoms linked to acid-base imbalance.

A root cause approach after a normal carbon dioxide result focuses on sustaining the systems that protect acid-base balance through lifestyle, nutrition, and preventive care. Periodic retesting through basic metabolic panels and functional assessments of overall health can ensure early detection of any shifts, helping to maintain acid-base balance over the long term.
