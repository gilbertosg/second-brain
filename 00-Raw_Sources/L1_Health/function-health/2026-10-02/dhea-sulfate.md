## Why it matters

Dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (DHEA-S) is a hormone primarily produced by the adrenal glands, two small organs about the size of a fortune cookie, located on top of the kidneys. It is classified as an androgen, a type of sex hormone involved in male traits and reproduction, and serves as a building block for testosterone and dihydrotestosterone (DHT), which are key male hormones. Beyond its role in reproduction, DHEA-S influences energy levels, mood, immune health, metabolism, blood pressure, and overall hormone balance. Its levels naturally peak in early adulthood and decline with age, making it an important marker of both adrenal and metabolic health.

DHEA-S works in tandem with other hormones like testosterone, cortisol (stress hormone), and androstenedione to maintain hormonal stability in the body. Measuring DHEA-S alongside these markers can provide insights into adrenal function, stress responses, and hormonal balance. DHEA-S is also connected to metabolic health, as its levels are associated with insulin sensitivity and body composition. Clinicians often use DHEA-S testing to guide decisions about addressing adrenal imbalances, male fertility concerns, or even cognitive performance in aging males.

- **Elevated DHEA-S** levels may suggest overactive adrenal glands or conditions like congenital adrenal hyperplasia.
- **Low DHEA-S** levels may point to adrenal insufficiency, aging, or chronic illness.

Addressing abnormal DHEA-S levels involves identifying and managing the root cause like chronic stress, inflammation, or metabolic imbalances. High levels may benefit from stress management and anti-inflammatory strategies, while low levels often respond to adrenal support through nutrients such as magnesium and vitamin C. Lifestyle adjustments, targeted supplementation, and comprehensive lab testing are key to restoring hormonal balance and supporting adrenal health.

**Summary**

DHEA-S is a male reproductive hormone (androgen) produced by the adrenal glands that serves as a precursor to testosterone and dihydrotestosterone (DHT), playing a key role in energy, mood, metabolism, and reproductive health. Abnormal levels may indicate adrenal dysfunction or other health issues, with testing providing valuable insights into hormonal balance and fertility.

**Optimal ranges**

The optimal range for DHEA-S may differ from standard laboratory reference ranges, with higher levels considered optimal for younger males (500-640 mcg/dL) and mid-range levels (400-500 mcg/dL) seen as ideal for middle-aged men.

For older males, maintaining levels above the lower threshold (300-400 mcg/dL) may help support vitality and reduce age-related decline. Even if levels fall within the standard reference range, lower levels may be considered suboptimal for some individuals, particularly if symptoms like fatigue, reduced libido, or low mood are present, emphasizing the importance of addressing lifestyle factors, stress, and nutrient deficiencies to support adrenal and hormonal health.

**Why does this biomarker fluctuate**

DHEA-S levels can naturally fluctuate because they’re influenced by age, stress, sleep, medications (e.g., glucocorticoids), blood sugar, and even the time of day. For example, chronic stress or poor sleep can suppress adrenal function and lower DHEA-S, while intense exercise can temporarily raise it. Since DHEA-S is more stable than other adrenal hormones, it's often preferred for testing, but lab values may vary slightly from day to day depending on lifestyle or health status. It’s important to interpret DHEA-S levels in the context of clinical symptoms, stress levels, and other hormones.

## Summary

A normal DHEA-S result means that DHEA-S levels are within the expected range of a person's age and health. DHEA-S is a hormone made primarily by the adrenal glands and plays a key role in producing testosterone, regulating metabolism, and supporting immune function. Normal levels suggest healthy adrenal function and balanced hormones, which are important for both physical and mental well-being.

From a root cause perspective, preserving healthy DHEA-S levels involves addressing lifestyle, nutrition, and environmental factors that influence hormonal balance. Eating a nutrient-rich diet with healthy fats (like avocados, nuts, and omega-3 fish), lean proteins, and antioxidant-rich fruits and vegetables can help the adrenal glands function well. Regular exercise improves metabolism and supports hormone health. Stress management techniques, such as mindfulness, yoga, or deep breathing, can reduce cortisol levels and protect the adrenal glands from stress-related issues. Staying hydrated, getting 7-9 hours of good-quality sleep, and reducing exposure to toxins, like those in plastics or pesticides, also help maintain hormone stability. Routine check-ups and lab tests to monitor hormone levels can catch potential problems early, ensuring long-term adrenal and hormonal health.

## Foods to limit

Inflammatory foods, such as alcohol, caffeine, sugar, starch, and processed food, can impact the health of hormones and vital systems, including adrenal glands, so it’s best to limit these foods to sustain healthy balance.
