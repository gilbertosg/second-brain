## Why it matters

Antinuclear antibodies (ANAs) are proteins made by your immune system that mistakenly targets your own cells. In a healthy immune system, antibodies are designed to protect you from infections. But when ANAs are found to be circulating in your blood, it can signal that the immune system is becoming misdirected and reacting against the body’s own tissues. Measuring ANA helps screen for autoimmune diseases like lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, Sjögren’s syndrome.

Monitoring ANA over time can help clinicians intervene early, guide next steps, and personalize care to better manage symptoms and slow disease progression. That said, ANA is rarely interpreted on its own and most meaningful when viewed within the broader clinical picture. If ANA is positive, clinicians often look at more specific antibody tests (like anti-dsDNA), along with inflammation markers such as high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) or erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), to better understand whether there is active immune system dysfunction or whether symptoms may be driven by another cause.

From a root cause perspective, it can also prompt a deeper look into triggers of an overactive immune system, including infections, stress, environmental exposures, and gut health. Supporting the body with an anti-inflammatory diet, regular physical activity, restorative sleep, stress management, and targeted supplements can help reduce these inflammatory triggers and promote immune balance.

- **If ANA is negative:** This is generally reassuring and suggests no significant autoimmune activity detected. However, some autoimmune conditions can still be present.
- **If ANA is positive:** Higher levels are more strongly associated with autoimmune conditions like lupus or Sjögren’s syndrome, particularly when symptoms such as joint pain, fatigue, rashes, or dry eyes/mouth are present.

**Summary**

Antinuclear antibodies (ANAs) help screen for autoimmune diseases, where your immune system may be attacking your body's own cells. A comprehensive root-cause approach includes routine monitoring of ANA levels to help guide a deeper look at triggers like stress, infections, and gut health that may be driving immune imbalance.

**Optimal ranges**

A ***negative*** ANA result is considered optimal, as it indicates that no or very low levels of these antibodies were detected. However, results should always be interpreted in the context of your symptoms, medical history, and other markers of immune function to get a complete picture of your health.

## Summary

A negative ANA (antinuclear antibody) screen is generally a reassuring result, and means that no significant levels of antibodies mistakenly targeting your cells were detected in your blood.

A negative ANA lowers the likelihood of certain autoimmune diseases like lupus, but does not completely rule out an autoimmune disease. Some autoimmune conditions can still be present even with a negative ANA, particularly in early stages or when the disease affects only one area of the body rather than the whole system.

If you are experiencing symptoms like persistent fatigue, joint pain, rashes, or dry eyes or mouth, or if other lab results like persistently elevated hs-CRP suggests ongoing inflammation, it is important to share your ANA results with your clinician along with any new or worsening symptoms. Keeping track of your symptoms over time by noting them in a journal when they occur, how often, and whether they are getting worse and affecting your quality of life can be useful. This is especially important if there is a strong suspicion of an autoimmune condition.

## Symptoms

Possible symptoms of in range ANA may include:

- **Dry eyes and mouth:** Symptoms commonly associated with SS.
- **Fatigue:** Persistent tiredness not relieved by rest.
- **Fever:** Unexplained or recurrent fevers.
- **Hair loss:** A common symptom in various autoimmune diseases.
- **Joint pain and swelling:** Often seen in RA and SLE.
- **Muscle pain:** Can be a sign of conditions like polymyositis.
- **Numbness or tingling:** Can occur in conditions like multiple sclerosis or SLE.
- **Sensitivity to sunlight:** Photosensitivity, particularly in SLE.
- **Skin rashes:** Such as the butterfly-shaped rash across the cheeks and nose in SLE.

These symptoms indicate an autoimmune response. If symptoms persist with low or undetectable ANA, a healthcare provider may suggest other tests, like checking for DNA (ds) antibodies, rheumatoid factor (RF), Sjögren’s antibodies (SS-A and SS-B), and other autoimmune biomarkers, which can be added on through Function.
