## Why it matters

Prolactin is a hormone made by the pituitary gland, a small structure at the base of the brain. While it is most commonly associated with stimulating milk production in women, prolactin also plays a role in men’s health by influencing hormones, metabolism, and immune function. Prolactin interacts with other systems in the body, acting like a “signal manager” to ensure balance across various processes.

Prolactin levels are closely tied to testosterone, as elevated prolactin can suppress testosterone production, which impacts energy, muscle mass, and libido. It also influences other biomarkers like follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH), which are important for sperm production and testicular function. Measuring prolactin provides insights into how hormonal systems are interacting and can guide clinical decisions in cases of sexual dysfunction, infertility, or unexplained fatigue. Additionally, it impacts mood and mental health, partly through its interaction with dopamine, a brain chemical associated with pleasure and motivation.

- **High prolactin (hyperprolactinemia)** : Elevated levels may indicate conditions such as a prolactinoma (a benign pituitary tumor), hypothyroidism, or the side effects of medications like antipsychotics. Symptoms might include low sex drive, erectile dysfunction, infertility, mood changes like anxiety or depression or breast tissue growth in men (gynecomastia).
- **Low prolactin** : While less common, low levels may suggest underlying metabolic issues, autoimmune conditions, or problems with the pituitary gland. Symptoms may include fatigue, reduced immune function, or hormonal imbalances.

Prolactin testing offers a window into the broader hormonal system. By identifying whether prolactin is out of balance, healthcare providers can uncover root causes of symptoms and develop tailored strategies to restore balance. This might include dietary changes, managing stress, adjusting medications, or treating specific conditions like hypothyroidism or prolactinomas. Prolactin is more than just a hormone; it is a vital marker for overall hormonal and metabolic health.

**Optimal ranges**

Though the normal range for prolactin in males is generally considered to be between 2-18 ng/mL, the optimal range of prolactin for men is not universally defined. However, a prolactin level of 5 ng/mL is considered a healthy baseline level.

## Summary

A normal prolactin level suggests that the glands in the brain responsible for regulating hormones, like the hypothalamus and pituitary gland, are functioning properly. This balance supports important processes like immune health, reproduction, and hormone signaling in the brain. Stable prolactin levels also reflect healthy dopamine activity, which controls prolactin production. While normal levels typically don't cause any symptoms, prolactin can naturally fluctuate during the day and may be influenced by stress, exercise, or certain foods.

Maintaining healthy prolactin levels involves supporting overall hormonal balance. Regular exercise, stress management techniques like meditation, and maintaining a healthy sleep schedule can help regulate prolactin production. Nutrients like zinc and vitamin B6, found in foods such as lean meats, legumes, and whole grains, can also support the glands that control prolactin production. Monitoring prolactin levels alongside related hormones through periodic testing can help ensure balance and catch any potential changes early.
