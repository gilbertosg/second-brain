## Why it matters

Vitamin D is a nutrient the body uses to absorb calcium and build strong bones, but it also functions more like a hormone. It affects how cells grow, how the immune system responds to threats, and even how the brain regulates mood. Unlike most vitamins, the body can make vitamin D when the skin is exposed to sunlight. However, many people don’t get enough of it, especially those living in areas with limited sun, spending most of their time indoors, or having darker skin tones, which reduces the skin’s ability to produce vitamin D from sunlight.

Vitamin D interacts with several other nutrients and hormones that help regulate mineral balance, inflammation, and bone metabolism. It works closely with calcium and phosphorus to support skeletal health and with parathyroid hormone (PTH) to maintain proper calcium levels in the blood. When vitamin D is low, PTH can rise to try to compensate, potentially weakening bones over time. Low vitamin D can also increase the body’s inflammatory response and affect levels of sex hormones, thyroid function, and insulin sensitivity. Measuring vitamin D gives insight into how well these systems are functioning and can guide decisions about diet, supplementation, and sun exposure. Vitamin D, through its active form binding to the vitamin D receptor, regulates the expression of over 900 genes involved in immune function, cell growth, hormone signaling, and detoxification. This broad gene regulation helps explain vitamin D’s wide-ranging effects on multiple body systems beyond bone health.

- **Low levels (below range)** may reflect poor calcium absorption and are linked to weakened bones, increased risk of osteoporosis, and reduced immune defense against infections.
- **High levels (above range)** are less common but can occur with over-supplementation and may lead to calcium buildup in the blood, which can cause nausea, kidney issues, or confusion.

Maintaining optimal vitamin D levels protects long-term bone health and strengthens the body’s ability to fight off illness.

## Summary

An in-range vitamin D level suggests the body has enough of this nutrient to support calcium absorption, bone strength, and immune regulation. Adequate levels are linked to better protection against osteoporosis, improved muscle function, and a more balanced immune response. Vitamin D also supports healthy inflammation control and may play a role in metabolic and hormone regulation. Maintaining levels within the reference range often reflects a balance of sun exposure, dietary intake, absorption, and overall health status. Continuing to support vitamin D through lifestyle, nutrition, and periodic testing can help sustain benefits and reduce the risk of future deficiency.

## Causes

Low levels of vitamin D may occur for several reasons:

- Advanced age
- Certain medications
- Issues absorbing vitamins and nutrients in the intestines. This is often a result of ulcerative colitis, Crohn’s disease, or celiac disease.
- Lack of sun exposure
- Osteoporosis
- Weight loss surgery

## Symptoms

Symptoms that may occur if vitamin D levels fall include:

- Bone pain
- Muscle pain
- Muscle weakness
- Tingly sensation in hands or feet

## Self care

Although vitamin D is obtained naturally through sun exposure, there are more efficient ways to elevate levels when deficient, including a balance between getting sun and vitamin D supplementation.
