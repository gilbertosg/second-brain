## Why it matters

Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) is a blood test that helps show how well the kidneys are working to remove waste and extra fluid from the body. The kidneys act as the body’s natural filters, and eGFR estimates how much blood moves through these tiny filters called glomeruli each minute. The result is calculated using creatinine levels in the blood, along with age, sex, and some other factors, offering a simple way to check kidney function.

The most common type of eGFR is creatinine-based, meaning it uses the level of creatinine—a waste product made by muscles—to estimate kidney filtration. While helpful, this type of eGFR can be affected by muscle mass, diet, and other factors. Cystatin C-based eGFR offers an alternative that may be more accurate in certain situations, as cystatin C levels are less influenced by muscle mass.

eGFR is often used alongside other kidney-related tests, such as serum creatinine, the urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR), and cystatin C. When used together, these tests can help identify kidney disease early, track changes over time, and guide decisions about medications or next steps in care. eGFR also plays a role in determining the stage of chronic kidney disease and when a referral to a kidney specialist may be needed.

- An eGFR below 60 for three months or more signals chronic kidney disease (CKD), and values below 15 indicate severe kidney impairment, sometimes called kidney failure.

**Summary**

The eGFR is a blood test that shows how well the kidneys are filtering waste from the blood, helping catch kidney problems early so the root causes, like high blood pressure or diabetes, can be addressed early before they get worse. By tracking eGFR over time, it’s may be possible to spot changes in kidney function, figure out what’s causing the decline, and take steps—such as improving your diet, managing blood sugar, or controlling blood pressure—to protect kidney health for the long term.

**Optimal ranges**

The optimal range for eGFR is generally considered to be 90-120 mL/min/1.73m<sup>2</sup>. This range indicates normal kidney function in most adults. However, it's important to note that eGFR naturally declines with age, even in healthy individuals.

**What makes this biomarker fluctuate**

eGFR results can change from day to day because they are influenced by many factors beyond just kidney health. Things like hydration status, recent exercise, diet (especially protein intake), body weight, certain medications, and even temporary illnesses or infections can all cause eGFR to go up or down in the short term. Age, muscle mass, and body composition also play a role, and people with higher baseline eGFR or obesity may see more fluctuation in their results. Because of these influences, eGFR can vary from one test to the next, so doctors look at trends over time rather than a single result to understand your true kidney function. This is why it’s normal for eGFR to fluctuate, and a temporary dip or rise doesn’t always mean your kidney health has changed significantly.

## Summary

An estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) in the normal range usually means the kidneys are working well. A value of 90 mL/min/1.73 m² or higher is considered normal and shows that the kidneys are effectively filtering waste from the blood. For adults over 70, an eGFR between 60 and 89 can still be normal, since kidney function naturally declines with age. A healthy eGFR generally suggests the tiny kidney filters—called glomeruli—are doing their job and that chronic kidney disease is unlikely.
