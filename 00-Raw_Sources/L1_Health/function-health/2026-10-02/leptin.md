## Why it matters

Leptin is a hormone made by the body’s fat cells that plays a major role in controlling hunger and metabolism. Think of leptin as the body’s “fuel gauge.” When fat stores are full, leptin sends a signal to the brain that says, “We’re good—no need to eat more right now.” On the other hand, when leptin levels are low, the brain gets the message that energy stores are low, triggering hunger. This system helps maintain a healthy balance between how much energy you take in and how much your body burns. However, when this system gets out of sync—such as in obesity or leptin resistance–the brain doesn’t register leptin's signal of fullness, so you may continue to feel hungry even when you have enough energy stored. This can lead to overeating and weight gain because the normal feedback loop that tells your body to stop eating isn’t working properly. Men with leptin resistance often have visceral obesity around abdominal organs and insulin resistance, driven by testosterone’s interaction with fat tissue.

Leptin and insulin work together to regulate blood sugar and energy metabolism, and when these hormones aren’t functioning properly, it can signal metabolic issues. Measuring leptin along with markers like testosterone, fasting insulin, glucose, and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) provides a clearer picture of metabolic health, helping to identify risks for obesity, insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, and heart disease. For example, testosterone reduces insulin-leptin crosstalk, increasing cardiovascular risk.

- **High leptin:** High leptin levels are often linked to increased belly fat, metabolic syndrome, and low testosterone. This happens because the brain doesn't respond properly to leptin, a condition called leptin resistance. It’s also commonly associated with insulin resistance and chronic inflammation, which increase the risk of metabolic problems and heart disease.
- **Low leptin:** Low leptin in men is often linked to low body fat or being underweight. In these cases, low leptin levels can lead to higher testosterone, as the body signals a need for reproductive function when any energy is available. However, if leptin is very low due to extreme calorie restriction, malnutrition, or a drastic energy deficit, it can cause a drop in testosterone, leading to conditions like hypogonadism (where the testes produce too little or no sex hormones) and muscle loss.

**Summary**

Leptin is a hormone made by fat cells that works like a fuel gauge, helping to control hunger and metabolism by signaling the brain when the body has enough energy stored and when you need to eat more. When leptin levels are too high or too low, it can signal issues like obesity or being underweight, and measuring it along with insulin, blood sugar, and testosterone can provide a more complete picture of metabolic balance.

**What makes this biomarker fluctuate**

Leptin concentrations in the blood, typically measured in serum, can vary significantly based on physiological and behavioral factors. This hormone follows a natural rhythm; it tends to rise at night and drop in the early morning. Sleep quality, stress levels, calorie intake, and physical activity can all cause short-term fluctuations. Even a single day of reduced eating or fasting can lower leptin, while factors like inflammation, thyroid issues, or hormone changes may also influence its levels. In men, higher belly fat leads to greater leptin resistance and more pronounced fluctuations. Testosterone suppresses leptin sensitivity, so changes in testosterone (such as with aging or low production in hypogonadism) can affect leptin regulation. Because of these shifts, one leptin measurement may not fully reflect long-term energy balance, so doctors often look at trends over time along with other metabolic markers.

## Summary

A leptin level within the normal range generally indicates healthy fat stores and balanced energy regulation. This suggests that the body is effectively producing and responding to leptin signals, which help control hunger, metabolism, and hormone balance. Normal leptin levels are associated with stable body weight, balanced blood sugar, and proper reproductive and immune function.

To maintain healthy leptin levels and improve overall metabolic function, a root cause approach focuses on supporting hormone balance, reducing inflammation, and optimizing nutrient intake. Consuming a balanced diet rich in healthy fats, lean proteins, and fiber can promote stable leptin signaling. Omega-3 fatty acids may reduce inflammation and improve leptin sensitivity by modulating cell membranes. Botanicals such as ashwagandha can support stress management, which helps regulate cortisol, a hormone that can suppress leptin if elevated. Ensuring adequate sleep, maintaining muscle mass through resistance training, and mindful eating can support leptin sensitivity and metabolic health.

## Foods to eat

Because leptin is made by fat cells, it's important to ensure those fats are healthy, such as those found in olive or avocado oil, nuts, and lean proteins.

Include fruits like avocados; nuts such as almonds, Brazil nuts, cashews, hazelnuts, macadamias, pecans, pistachios, and walnuts; and a variety of oils, including almond oil, avocado oil, extra virgin olive oil, flaxseed oil, grass-fed ghee (clarified butter), hemp oil, macadamia oil, sesame seed oil, tahini (sesame seed paste), virgin coconut oil, and walnut oil.

Additionally, consume wild or sustainable coldwater fish like anchovies, herring, mackerel, sardines, and wild salmon. For meat options, consider beef, bison, chicken, elk, lamb, turkey, and venison.

## Foods to limit

Limiting refined sugars, starches, and ultra-processed foods can help limit the chances of inflammation from beginning and/or becoming chronic.

## Self care

Regular physical activity effectively reduces bodily inflammation.

Ensuring adequate, high-quality sleep supports the body's ability to combat inflammation.

Stress management techniques such as meditation, yoga, breathwork, and other mindfulness practices contribute to inflammation reduction.

Minimizing exposure to environmental toxins in household cleaning products, avoiding highly polluted areas, and being cautious of mercury levels can optimize body function and lessen inflammation triggers.
