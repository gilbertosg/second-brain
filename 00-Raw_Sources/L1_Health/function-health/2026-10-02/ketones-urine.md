## Why it matters

Ketones are produced in the liver and are part of the body’s metabolic processes. They come from the burning of fat and the breakdown of fatty acids into a compound called acetyl CoA, which is then converted in the liver to ketones and exported to cells as a fuel source. Ketones are formed when there’s not enough glucose or sugar to fuel the body’s energy needs during fasting or when consuming a ketogenic diet. Keto is 70-80% fat and less than 5% carbohydrates.

Cells are like a hybrid engine. They can run on glucose (gas), or fat in the form of ketones (electric). When dietary glucose is low from fasting or from a ketogenic diet, the body can break down fat stores and use them for energy.

Ketones in the urine may also be a sign of untreated or uncontrolled diabetes that creates a serious metabolic imbalance called ketoacidosis. This most commonly occurs in type 1 diabetes mellitus, but may also occur in very poorly controlled type 2 diabetes.

Ketones can be found in both blood and urine. This test only tests for ketones in urine.

## Foods to eat

Overall, a well-balanced, whole-food diet is beneficial for healthy ketone levels.

- Grass-fed, regeneratively- or pasture-raised meats, poultry, and fish (if accessible).
- Good fats, such as olive oil, avocados, nuts, and seeds.
- Vegetables (all except for starchy root vegetables, like potatoes, which are best eaten in moderation).
- High-fiber, prebiotic, and probiotic foods are helpful to the microbiome, an important system that helps regulate blood glucose.

**Time-restricted eating is also helpful in improving metabolic control.**

- Avoid eating three hours before bed.
- Leave at least 12 hours—ideally 14 to 16 hours—between dinner and breakfast.

**Order of eating foods**

- Eat vegetables, fat, and protein before any carbohydrates.
- Start the day with protein and fat for breakfast. Try reducing or avoiding carbohydrates or sugar at breakfast.
- Avoid bread, alcohol, or any refined carbohydrate foods before eating vegetables, fat, or protein in a meal.
