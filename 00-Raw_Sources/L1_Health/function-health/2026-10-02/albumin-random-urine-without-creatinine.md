## Why it matters

Albumin is a key protein in your blood that normally stays inside your circulation. When it shows up in urine (as microalbumin), it can be an early sign that the kidneys’ filtering system is being affected and potentially damaged.

In early kidney disease, often driven by high blood pressure or type 2 diabetes, small amounts of albumin begin to “leak” through the kidney’s filters into the urine. This can happen before standard kidney markers like creatinine or BUN become abnormal, making urine albumin testing one of the earliest ways to detect kidney stress, particularly in someone with diabetes or at risk for chronic kidney disease (CKD).

A random urine microalbumin without creatinine has limited clinical utility, without a follow up urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR). Therefore, the results of this biomarker should not be interpreted on its own
