## Why it matters

Eosinophils are called to inflamed areas to trap substances, kill cells, parasites, and bacteria, and help tame an allergic response. This white blood cell (WBC) is best known for triggering allergy symptoms and often gets pretty enthusiastic about its job by mounting an immune response against something harmless. For example, eosinophils mistake pollen for a foreign invader, and people who react to pollen trigger an overactive eosinophil response.

Eosinophils account for no more than 5% of the WBCs in your bloodstream. This WBC can sometimes exceed levels in various parts of the body, typically the digestive tract.

However, eosinophils’ main targets are viruses, bacteria, fungi, intestinal worms, or other parasites in the digestive tract.

## Summary

Your eosinophils are within a healthy range. To maintain a well-working immune army, nourish the microbiome, and try to get in regular exercise and adequate amounts of sleep.

## Foods to eat

Eating a whole food, unprocessed diet rich in anti-inflammatory phytonutrients found in colorful vegetables and fruits and rich in fiber and omega-3 fats is incredibly beneficial for immunity biomarkers, such as eosinophils.

## Foods to limit

Limiting refined sugars, starches, and ultra-processed foods can support balanced immune activity and help keep eosinophil levels within a healthy range.

## Self care

Regular exercise helps keep white blood cells strong.

Getting enough good quality sleep can help the body ward off potential infection and/or fight off inflammation.

Managing stress through meditation, yoga, breathwork, and other mindfulness practices.

Trying to avoid environmental toxins in home cleaning products, highly polluted regions, mercury levels, and more can help your body function at optimal levels and reduce causes of inflammation.
