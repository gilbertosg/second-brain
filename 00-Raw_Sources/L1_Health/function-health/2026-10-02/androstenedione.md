## Why it matters

Androstenedione is a naturally occurring hormone made mainly in the adrenal glands and testes, and it acts as a "precursor" for other important hormones like testosterone and estrogen, called the androgen pathway. Because it is essential to the androgen pathway, helping maintain muscle mass, bone density, and libido---measuring androstenedione can provide a window into how the body is balancing sex hormone production.

Androstenedione doesn't work in isolation---it interacts with other hormones such as dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), testosterone, and estradiol, creating a hormonal "network" that influences metabolism, sexual function, muscle mass, and even mood. Because androstenedione can be converted to either testosterone or estrogen, its levels may indicate shifts in these related hormones. Clinically, abnormal androstenedione levels can help detect conditions such as adrenal disorders, Cushing's syndrome, and even metabolic issues tied to obesity and inflammation. This makes it a valuable biomarker for guiding treatment decisions, monitoring disease, and distinguishing between adrenal and testicular causes of androgen imbalance.

- **Low androstenedione** : Can be seen in adrenal insufficiency (when the adrenal glands don't make enough hormones) or hypogonadism (underactive testes in men), potentially contributing to symptoms such as low energy, muscle weakness, and decreased libido. Low levels can also point to impaired hormone pathways, as seen in men undergoing androgen deprivation therapy for prostate cancer.
- **High androstenedione** : Elevated levels may signal overactivity of the adrenal glands like adrenal hyperplasia, involving enzyme changes in steroid metabolism, or androgen-secreting tumors. High androstenedione levels might contribute to symptoms like acne, hair loss, or (more indirectly) increased risk of metabolic disorders.

**Summary**

Androstenedione is a key hormone the body uses to produce testosterone and other androgens that support muscle strength, mood, and vitality. When it's off balance, it can be a sign the adrenal glands or hormone pathways may be under stress. A root cause approach may look at why the hormone system is imbalanced---whether from chronic stress, nutrient deficiencies, or inflammation---so natural hormone balance can be restored.

**Optimal ranges**

In practice, many laboratories consider a "normal" adult range to be about 40-190 ng/dL, depending on age. However, the optimal range might fluctuate, since ideal levels depend not only on age, but lab testing methods, and overall hormone balance, so the aim involves a more individualized approach based on one's health assessment. Some consider the optimal window to lie toward the middle part of the normal reference range---assuming other hormones (like testosterone, SHBG, estrogen, DHEA) are in good balance---because being too low might limit hormone conversion and being too high could reflect stress, inflammation, or excessive adrenal activity.

**What makes this biomarker fluctuate**

Androstenedione levels can fluctuate throughout the day because this hormone follows a circadian rhythm, meaning its production naturally rises and falls based on the body's internal clock---typically peaking in the morning and tapering off by evening, paralleling other steroid hormones such as cortisol and DHEA. These shifts can be influenced by several factors, including stress, which activates the adrenal glands and can temporarily alter androstenedione, sleep quality, which may affect hormone regulation, and dietary intake or fasting states that alter metabolism. However, in healthy men with stable adrenal and gonadal function, these fluctuations usually remain within a narrow physiological range and reflect adaptive flexibility rather than dysfunction.

## Summary

A normal androstenedione level suggests that the body is maintaining a balanced interaction between the adrenal glands and the testes, which together regulate the production of key steroid hormones including testosterone and estradiol. When androstenedione is in range, it typically reflects healthy adrenal and gonadal function, proper cholesterol metabolism, and stable signaling through the hypothalamic--pituitary--gonadal (HPG) axis. This balance supports reproductive function, energy, mood, and muscle integrity, while preventing symptoms like fatigue, low libido, or mood instability often seen in hormonal dysregulation.

From a root cause perspective, maintaining optimal androstenedione involves supporting adrenal resilience, nutrient status, and metabolic stability. Nutritional strategies may include increasing intake of zinc, magnesium, and vitamin D, which act as cofactors in hormone synthesis. Diets rich in omega-3 fatty acids and cruciferous vegetables may reduce inflammation and can help regulate estrogen--androgen balance. Follow-up testing might include evaluating DHEA, testosterone, cortisol, and fasting insulin to assess upstream influences on androgen metabolism. Together, this comprehensive approach can strengthen the body's resilience, helping to sustain androstenedione levels within their optimal range and prevent future hormonal decline.
