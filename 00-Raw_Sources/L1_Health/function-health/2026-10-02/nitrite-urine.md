## Why it matters

Healthy urine has chemicals called nitrates. If bacteria enter the urinary tract, nitrates can transform into a chemical called nitrites. Increased nitrites in urine, therefore, may be a sign of an infection in the urinary tract, a system that includes the kidneys, renal pelvis, ureters, bladder, and urethra. This is known as a urinary tract infection (UTI). Sometimes, however, not all bacteria are able to convert nitrate to nitrite, so it is possible to have a UTI even with a negative nitrite test.

## Summary

You're within a healthy range. Since your urinary tract covers a wide range of organs, it’s always important to support it by staying hydrated with water, urinating when you need to, and even fully emptying the bladder each time you go.

## Self care

Prevention and care require proper hygiene, including wiping from front to back, urination after sexual intercourse, adequate hydration, wearing loose-fitting underwear, and using a bidet instead of toilet paper to prevent urinary tract infections. There are a lot of bidets on the market now that can easily attach to your toilet.
