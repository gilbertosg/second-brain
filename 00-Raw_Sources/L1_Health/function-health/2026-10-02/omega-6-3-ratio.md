## Why it matters

The omega-6 to omega-3 ratio is a biomarker that reflects the body’s internal balance between pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory signaling. When this ratio is measured, it gives a snapshot of how the body might be managing—or struggling with—chronic, low-grade inflammation. Most people consuming a typical Western diet tend to have a ratio that’s higher than optimal, which can silently drive processes linked to heart disease, metabolic dysfunction, and even cognitive decline. Measuring it doesn’t just flag a dietary imbalance; it points to how well the body is equipped to handle stress, repair tissue, and prevent long-term disease.

This ratio complements other markers of inflammation and metabolic stress, such as hs-CRP, IL-6, fasting glucose, and triglycerides. Unlike those, however, it gives insight into the upstream factors—especially dietary habits and fat metabolism—that often contribute to dysfunction before more overt lab changes show up. For clinicians, it can guide decisions about adjusting omega-3 intake, addressing dietary sources of omega-6, or investigating fat absorption issues. It’s also useful for tracking progress when someone begins a therapeutic nutrition plan aimed at lowering inflammation.

**Why are the reference range and optimal ranges so different?**

Most labs set their ranges based on averages from the general population. However, the typical modern diet is heavily weighted toward omega-6 fats and low in omega-3s. This means most people have imbalanced ratios, sometimes as high as 10:1, 15:1, or even 20:1. Since the lab range reflects the average rather than what is optimal for health, the upper end of the “normal” range is often too high.

In the case of this ratio, research has shown that a lower omega-6 to omega-3 ratio—closer to 2:1 to 4:1—is linked to:

- Reduced chronic inflammation
- Lower risk of heart disease
- Better brain function and mood
- Healthier metabolism and immune regulation

**Optimal ranges**

The optimal ratio is 2.0-4.0, which means you’re getting nearly equal or slightly more omega-6 than omega-3. This supports anti-inflammatory balance and long-term health. If your ratio is lower than what the lab considers “normal,” that’s not a red flag—it’s a sign that you’re doing something right.

## Summary

As described above, the lab reference range for the omega-6 to omega-3 ratio is quite broad, often including values common in the general population but not necessarily ideal for long-term health. Most people today consume far more omega-6 than omega-3, which skews the average ratio high, which, in turn, pushes the lab’s “normal” range higher as well.

*For optimal health, however, research supports a tighter ratio of about 2.0 to 4.0. Because the result falls within the lab's reference range, it shows up normal, but if it’s above 4.0, there may be opportunities to improve.*

An omega-6 to omega-3 ratio in the normal range indicates a healthy balance between pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory fatty acids, suggesting that the body can respond to injury or infection without tipping into chronic inflammation. Because the optimal range and reference range are so different, below is information that can be beneficial if your range falls above what is optimal for health.

## Causes

**Root causes of omega-6 to omega-3 ratio above the optimal range**

This often reflects deeper imbalances in diet, metabolism, and inflammation that shift the body toward a more pro-inflammatory state. Root causes may include excessive intake of LA- and AA-rich foods, insufficient omega-3 consumption, chronic stress, metabolic dysfunction, and impaired fatty acid processing or absorption.

**Diet and lifestyle factors**

- **Dietary imbalances** : A diet high in LA-rich foods like refined seed oils and processed snacks, combined with AA-rich sources such as red and processed meats, while low in omega-3 fatty acids, can contribute to an elevated ratio. Excessive omega-6 intake, particularly from refined seed and vegetable oils and processed foods, can promote inflammation when not balanced with sufficient omega-3s.
- **Increased stress:** Chronic stress may worsen inflammation, impair metabolic function, and exacerbate the inflammatory effects of a high omega-6 intake.
- **Sedentary lifestyle:** Limited physical activity may impair the body’s ability to regulate inflammation and fatty acid balance.

**Other factors**

- **Genetic predisposition** : Some individuals may have genetic variations that affect the metabolism of fatty acids.
- **Hormonal imbalances** : Conditions like insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome can influence fatty acid metabolism, promoting systemic inflammation.
- **Oxidative stress** : High levels of oxidative stress can trigger the release of certain fatty acids from cell membranes, increasing the production of inflammatory compounds that contribute to chronic inflammation.
- **Poor gut health** : Imbalances in the gut microbiome or conditions like "leaky gut" may trigger systemic inflammation by increasing the release of inflammatory mediators.
- **Obesity and excess adipose tissue** : Excess body fat, particularly visceral fat, produces inflammatory cytokines that can amplify inflammatory processes.

**Medications**

**​** Based on current peer-reviewed human clinical and pharmacological studies, there are no medications that have been conclusively shown to directly lower the omega-6 to omega-3 ratio as a primary pharmacodynamic action.

**Substance use**

Substance use can disrupt fatty balance and exacerbate the effects of a higher omega-6 to omega-3 ratio through several mechanisms:

- **Alcohol:** Chronic alcohol intake can impair the liver’s ability to metabolize and convert essential fatty acids, leading to imbalanced levels. Alcohol also affects nutrient absorption and may displace dietary intake of omega-3-rich foods.
- **Smoking:** Smoking is associated with increased oxidative stress and inflammation, which can disrupt fatty acid metabolism. Additionally, smoking can negatively affect nutritional status, including fatty acid balance.

## Associated conditions

**General conditions and diseases**

An omega-6 to omega-3 ratio that is above the optimal range could potentially indicate a pro-inflammatory shift, which has been linked to a range of chronic and inflammatory conditions.

- **Cardiovascular disease:** Elevated omega-6 to omega-3 ratio is associated with increased risk of heart disease, high blood pressure, and elevated triglycerides. A more balanced ratio helps lower triglycerides, support arterial function, and reduce cardiovascular risk.
- **Arthritis** : Higher omega-6 to omega-3 ratio may worsen inflammatory joint conditions like rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis by amplifying local inflammation.
- **Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)** : Conditions such as Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis may be aggravated by an elevated ratio that contributes to gut inflammation.
- **Metabolic syndrome** : A higher omega-6 to omega-3 ratio has been linked to insulin resistance and obesity, raising the risk for type 2 diabetes.
- **Neurodegenerative diseases:** Increased risk of cognitive decline, including Alzheimer's disease and other forms of dementia, is associated with a lower omega-3 index. Adequate omega-3 levels support cognitive functions such as memory and learning, and deficiencies are linked to neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer's.

**Mental health conditions**

- **Depression:** Low omega-3 levels have been linked to an increased risk of depressive symptoms and mood disorders.
- **Anxiety:** Imbalances in fatty acid composition, including insufficient omega-3s, may contribute to greater susceptibility to anxiety and stress-related symptoms.
- **Schizophrenia:** Low omega-3 levels may increase the risk of schizophrenia or worsen its symptoms.

## Foods to eat

Nutrition plays a key role in overall health. [Nutrition 101](https://www.functionhealth.com/guide/nutrition-101) is a science-based review of foundational nutritional principles and eating practices that promote health and well-being. It is founded on three core principles:

- **Whole real food** : Eat real, whole, or minimally processed foods and avoid ultra-processed foods.
- **Food is medicine** : All food constituents, including protein, fat, carbohydrates, fiber, phytochemicals, vitamins, and minerals, regulate every process and cellular function in the body.
- **Personalization** : Age, sex, genetics, preferences, health conditions, and individual goals should guide the optimal diet for every unique individual.

Using food as medicine to support a healthy omega-6 to omega-3 ratio focuses on incorporating nutrient-dense foods that provide essential fatty acids while addressing imbalances that drive inflammation.

- **Balancing omega-6 and omega-3 intake** :
  - Including omega-3-rich foods like fatty fish, flaxseeds, chia seeds, and walnuts while consuming healthy omega-6 sources such as nuts and seeds in moderation helps maintain an optimal fatty acid ratio and supports brain health.
- **Cruciferous vegetables (e.g., broccoli, kale, Brussels sprouts)** : These vegetables contain compounds like sulforaphane and indole-3-carbinol that enhance detoxification and modulate inflammation by influencing the metabolic pathways that convert arachidonic acid (AA) into inflammatory eicosanoids.
- **Berries (e.g., blueberries, strawberries)** : High in antioxidants such as quercetin and anthocyanins, berries can help reduce oxidative stress and inflammation.
- **Spices like turmeric and ginger** : Turmeric and ginger contain compounds that inhibit COX-2 and LOX enzymes, reducing the formation of inflammatory eicosanoids from AA. Curcumin in turmeric and gingerols in ginger can be taken daily to support inflammatory balance.
- **Foods that support gut health**
- **Probiotics and prebiotics** : A healthy gut microbiome can enhance nutrient absorption and modulate inflammation. Probiotic-rich foods like kefir and fermented vegetables, and prebiotic-rich foods like onions, garlic, and bananas support gut health.
- **Fiber-rich foods** : Foods high in fiber, such as fruits, vegetables, and whole grains, support digestive health and can improve the absorption of nutrients.

## Foods to limit

Reducing inflammatory and nutrient-depleting foods is important while supporting the body's ability to restore balanced fatty acid levels.

- **Trans fats and hydrogenated oils (margarine, partially hydrogenated oils)** : These unhealthy fats negatively impact fatty acid metabolism.
- **Highly processed foods (e.g., fast food, snack foods, packaged baked goods):** These products are often rich in harmful additives and refined vegetable and seed oils that are high in linoleic acid (LA), an omega-6 fatty acid. While LA is essential in small amounts, excessive intake (common in modern diets) can drive the body toward producing more inflammatory compounds and contribute to an imbalanced omega-6 to omega-3 ratio.
- **Excessive red meat and processed meats (sausages, bacon, hot dogs)** : These foods are significant sources of arachidonic acid (AA), another omega-6 fatty acid derived from animal products. High AA intake can increase the production of pro-inflammatory molecules, potentially worsening omega-3 deficiency and contributing to chronic inflammatory states.

For more information, visit [Nutrition 101](https://www.functionhealth.com/guide/nutrition-101).

## Supplements

Supplements can play an important role in supporting overall health and well-being by providing essential nutrients that may be lacking in a regular diet, regulating hormones and metabolic processes, and contributing to the prevention and management of various conditions. [Supplements 101](https://www.functionhealth.com/guide/supplements-101) provides more information on supplements.

*Please remember, before introducing a new supplement, it’s wise to speak with your healthcare provider. If you have a medical condition or are on medication, this step helps you stay safe and make sure everything works together to support your health.*

**Targeted**

Targeted supplements may help address an omega-6 to omega-3 ratio by reducing inflammation, protecting omega-3 fats from oxidative damage, and supporting efficient fatty acid metabolism.

**Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA + DHA)**

- **Benefits** : Reduce inflammation and help balance the omega-3 to omega-6 ratio to support cardiovascular health.
- **Dosage** : 1,000-2,000 mg.
- **Frequency** : Daily.
- **Mechanism of action** : Provide EPA and DHA, which compete with omega-6 fatty acids for enzyme pathways, reducing the production of pro-inflammatory eicosanoids.

**Flaxseed oil**

- **Benefits:** Rich in alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), which can be converted to EPA and DHA, supports anti-inflammatory processes, and balances omega-3 to omega-6 levels.
- **Dosage:** 1,000-2,000 mg.
- **Frequency:** Daily.
- **Mechanism of action:** ALA is converted to EPA, DPA, and DHA, contributing to the omega-3 pool, and helping to balance omega-6 levels to have anti-inflammatory effects.

**Algal oil**

- **Benefits:** Provides a plant-based source of omega-3 fats, supports cognitive function, and reduces inflammation.
- **Dosage:** 200-500 mg of combined EPA and DHA.
- **Frequency:** Daily.
- **Mechanism of action:** Some algae oils are rich in EPA and DHA to boost omega-3 intake and help maintain omega-3 balance, supporting cellular functions and reducing inflammation.

**Krill oil**

- **Benefits:** Provides EPA and DHA in phospholipid form, supports cognitive and cardiovascular health, and reduces inflammation.
- **Dosage:** 500-1,000 mg.
- **Frequency:** Daily.
- **Mechanism of action:** Krill oil, extracted from small, shrimp-like crustaceans found mainly in the Antarctic Ocean, is rich in EPA and DHA and contains choline, which may improve absorption and support overall omega-3 status. Unlike fish oil and algae oil, krill oil features these fatty acids bound to phospholipids, which may improve their bioavailability, allowing for incorporation into cell membranes, improving omega-3 status, and reducing inflammation.

**Generalized**

Additional supplements may help reduce chronic inflammation by modulating inflammatory pathways and supporting metabolic health.

**Evening primrose oil**

- **Benefits:** Contains gamma-linolenic acid (GLA), supports skin health, and reduces inflammation.
- **Dosage:** 500-1,000 mg.
- **Frequency:** Daily.
- **Mechanism of action:** Provides GLA that converts to anti-inflammatory prostaglandins and helps balance omega-6 and omega-3 ratios.

**Vitamin D3**

- **Benefits:** Enhances omega-3 absorption and utilization and supports immune function and bone health.
- **Dosage:** 1,000-2,000 IU.
- **Frequency:** Daily with omega-3s.
- **Mechanism of action:** Aids in omega-3 fatty acid absorption and helps balance omega-3 and omega-6 levels.

**Curcumin**

- **Benefits:** Has potent anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, supports joint health, and may improve omega-3 efficacy.
- **Dosage:** 500-1,000 mg.
- **Frequency:** Daily.
- **Mechanism of action:** Inhibits inflammatory pathways and oxidative stress.

**Alpha-lipoic acid**

- **Benefits:** An antioxidant that may help reduce inflammation and improve insulin sensitivity.
- **Dosage:** 300-600 mg.
- **Frequency:** Once to twice daily.
- **Mechanism of action:** Neutralizes free radicals and enhances the regeneration of other antioxidants, reducing oxidative stress and inflammation.

**Probiotics**

- **Benefits:** Support gut health and may help reduce systemic inflammation.
- **Dosage:** 5-20 billion CFUs, depending on the specific strain and health goals.
- **Frequency:** Once daily.
- **Mechanism of action:** Modulate the immune system and help maintain gut integrity, reducing the systemic effects of inflammation.

**Milk thistle (*silybum marianum*)**

- **Benefits:** Known for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, particularly in liver health.
- **Dosage:** 140-600 mg of silymarin.
- **Frequency:** Two to three times daily.
- **Mechanism of action:** Protects against oxidative damage and reduces inflammation by modulating inflammatory pathways.

## Self care

**Exercise and movement**

Exercise can help improve an imbalanced omega-6 to omega-3 ratio by reducing inflammation, supporting metabolic health, and enhancing the body’s ability to regulate fatty acid balance. Moderate-intensity aerobic activities, such as walking, cycling, or swimming, can lower systemic inflammation and oxidative stress, which may reduce the pro-inflammatory burden driven by excess omega-6s. Resistance training, such as weightlifting, can enhance lipid metabolism, ensuring better utilization of dietary fats. Consistent, balanced physical activity supports the body’s ability to restore and maintain essential fatty acid levels.

For more detailed information on exercise, please refer to [Exercise 101](https://www.functionhealth.com/guide/exercise-101).

**Weight management**

Weight balance may help support a healthier omega-6 to omega-3 ratio by reducing chronic inflammation and promoting more efficient fatty acid metabolism. Improved insulin sensitivity, often associated with maintaining a balanced weight, also promotes more efficient processing of fatty acids. While weight balance doesn’t directly alter the omega-6 to omega-3 ratio, maintaining a healthy intake of both can support weight regulation by influencing hormones like leptin, which affect appetite, energy use, and fat storage.

Refer to [Weight Management 101](https://www.functionhealth.com/guide/weight-management-101) for foundational principles for healthy weight management.

**Sleep optimization**

Quality sleep is essential for regulating inflammation and supporting a healthy omega-6 to omega-3 ratio. Poor sleep increases the production of inflammatory cytokines and pro-inflammatory eicosanoids, which can amplify the effects of excess omega-6 activity and contribute to a higher, less favorable ratio. Restorative sleep also helps maintain insulin sensitivity and stable metabolic function, reducing disturbances that could exacerbate inflammation and disrupt the omega-6 to omega-3 ratio.

Visit [Sleep 101](https://www.functionhealth.com/guide/sleep-101) for practical tips on improving sleep.

**Mental health and stress reduction**

Mental health and stress reduction practices can help mitigate the inflammatory effects associated with a higher omega-6 to omega-3 ratio. Chronic stress increases inflammatory cytokines and promotes the conversion of linoleic acid (LA) into pro-inflammatory eicosanoids, which can elevate omega-6 activity and disrupt the fatty acid balance. Effective stress management strategies, such as mindfulness and relaxation techniques, have been shown to lower these inflammatory markers. Supporting mental health also improves metabolic function and insulin sensitivity, helping to reduce the inflammatory impact of excess omega-6 intake and maintain a healthier omega-6 to omega-3 ratio.

For more information on mental health and stress management, visit [Mental Health 101](https://www.functionhealth.com/guide/mental-health-101) and [Stress Management 101](https://www.functionhealth.com/guide/stress-management-101).

**Detoxification**

Detoxification may help regulate the omega-6 to omega-3 ratio by reducing systemic inflammation and supporting fatty acid metabolism. See [Environmental Health 101](https://www.functionhealth.com/guide/environmental-health-101).

**Gut health support**

Supporting gut health may help regulate the omega-6 to omega-3 ratio by enhancing fatty acid metabolism and absorption through a healthy microbiome. See [Gut Health 101](https://www.functionhealth.com/guide/gut-health-101).
