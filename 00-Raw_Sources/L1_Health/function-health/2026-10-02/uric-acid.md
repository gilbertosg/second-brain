## Why it matters

Uric acid is a natural waste product that forms when the body breaks down purines, which come from both the body's own cells and certain foods like red meat, seafood, and sugary drinks. Normally, the kidneys filter out excess uric acid, which is then excreted through urine. However, imbalances can occur when the body produces too much or the kidneys don’t remove enough, potentially leading to health concerns.

Uric acid is closely linked to metabolic health and kidney function, often tested alongside glucose, insulin, hemoglobin A1c, and kidney markers like creatinine. Because diet, hydration, and metabolic health influence uric acid levels, tracking this marker provides valuable insights into metabolic balance, kidney efficiency, and potential risks for related conditions.

- **Elevated levels of uric acid** **(hyperuricemia)** may contribute to inflammation and urate crystal formation, increasing the risk of gout that causes joint pain, kidney stones, and cardiovascular disease.
- **Low levels of uric acid (hypouricemia)** may be linked to malnutrition, genetic conditions affecting purine metabolism, or kidney dysfunction.

**Summary**

Uric acid levels are important for health because both high and low levels can signal underlying problems, such as gout, kidney stones, or nutrient deficiencies. A root cause approach focuses on identifying factors like diet, hydration, medications, and kidney function to address imbalances and prevent complications.

**Optimal ranges**

The optimal ranges for serum uric acid are generally considered to be:

**Male:** 4.0-5.5 mg/dL

**Female:** 3.5-5.0 mg/dL

These levels are associated with a lower risk of developing gout, cardiovascular and neurodegenerative diseases, and other related conditions.

**Why can this biomarker fluctuate?**

Uric acid levels can fluctuate due to a variety of factors, making it important to interpret test results in context rather than as a standalone. Diet plays a key role; eating foods high in purines, such as red meat, seafood, and sugary drinks, can temporarily raise uric acid levels, while staying well-hydrated helps the kidneys flush excess uric acid out of the body. Medications like diuretics or aspirin, alcohol intake, and even exercise can influence levels by affecting kidney function and metabolism of purines and uric acid. Additionally, stress and metabolic shifts, such as fasting or sudden weight loss, can temporarily impact uric acid balance. Since levels can vary from day to day, tracking trends over time provides a clearer picture of overall metabolic and kidney health rather than relying on a single test result.

## Summary

A uric acid level within the normal range indicates a healthy balance in purine metabolism and the body’s ability to regulate and excrete uric acid effectively. Normal uric acid levels suggest that processes like kidney function, cellular turnover, and antioxidant activity are functioning optimally to maintain metabolic health. Maintaining uric acid within the normal range is essential for supporting energy metabolism, immune function, and overall well-being.

Maintaining normal uric acid levels from a root cause perspective involves addressing lifestyle factors. Proper hydration and a balanced diet rich in purine-containing whole foods, such as lean proteins and legumes, can support uric acid production and metabolic balance. Ensuring adequate nutrient intake, including B vitamins and magnesium, supports purine metabolism and kidney function. By focusing on these foundational aspects of health, it is possible to support the body's natural ability to maintain healthy uric acid levels and overall metabolic health.
