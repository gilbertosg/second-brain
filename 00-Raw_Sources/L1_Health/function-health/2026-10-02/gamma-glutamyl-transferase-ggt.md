# Gamma-glutamyl Transferase (GGT)

An enzyme mainly found in the liver that helps protect cells from damage. A blood test for GGT can reveal early signs of liver problems.

## Why it matters

Gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT) is an enzyme mostly found in the liver and kidneys. GGT helps the body break down and recycle glutathione, which is one of the most powerful antioxidants the body makes. By doing this, GGT plays a supporting role in antioxidant defense, helping to limit oxidative stress and its damaging effects. By supporting these detoxification and defense systems, GGT helps protect the body and maintain inflammatory balance.

GGT functions as a metabolic “warning light,” indicating potential oxidative stress, cardiovascular changes, or irregularities with blood sugar and fat metabolism. As an early marker of liver damage, GGT can rise due to many factors and may signal risk for long-term conditions. Elevated levels have been linked to oxidative stress from air pollution and industrial chemical exposure, both of which place added detoxification demands on the liver.

GGT pairs with markers like bilirubin and aminotransferases (ALT, AST) to give a fuller snapshot of liver health. Tracking GGT over time can help spot ongoing liver strain, low antioxidant defenses (the body’s ability to fight cell damage), or increased risk of serious chronic illnesses, making it a valuable marker to monitor for overall health.

- **High GGT (above normal range):** Typically indicates liver stress, such as inflammation, fat accumulation (fatty liver), or gallstones blocking bile flow. Elevated GGT may also be associated with obesity, cardiovascular risks, and, less commonly, certain cancers, reflecting broader metabolic or cellular stress. It can also be a sign of environmental exposure from air pollution, industrial chemicals, heavy metals, and plastics.
- **Low GGT (below normal range):** Low GGT is rarely concerning and typically reflects lower oxidative stress and reduced risk of chronic diseases, though extremely low levels (e.g., <5 U/L for women or <7 U/L for men) in rare cases may indicate severe liver damage.

**Optimal ranges**

When GGT rises toward the higher end of the normal reference range, the risk of metabolic syndrome, impaired glucose handling, and coronary artery calcification goes up.

Based on this, GGT targets of approximately 5-17 U/L in men and 5-14 U/L in women may be most optimal. Levels above these ranges, even if still technically normal, may signal increased oxidative stress or broader cardiometabolic strain from factors such as alcohol, medications, fatty liver, or metabolic dysfunction and deserve a closer look with a clinician, especially in the context of other liver and metabolic tests. These targets apply to generally healthy, nonpregnant adults; in pregnancy or known liver disease, GGT needs more specialized interpretation.

**What makes this biomarker fluctuate**

GGT levels measured in blood can change from day to day because GGT responds to many factors, including diet, alcohol intake, medications, physical activity, and even air pollution. Certain medications and brief periods of elevated alcohol use can cause GGT to rise temporarily. Changes in liver health, weight, or exposure to chemicals can also shift GGT results, which is why one lab draw may be different from another taken months later. Tracking GGT over time gives a clearer picture than any one result alone.

## Summary

A normal gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT) test result generally indicates that there is likely no active liver damage involving this enzyme. GGT plays a key role in processing toxins and supporting the antioxidant glutathione. Most people with normal levels tend to be at lower risk for liver disease, cardiovascular issues, and complications related to metabolic stress. Keeping GGT in the normal range is best supported by a balanced diet with plenty of colorful fruits and vegetables, healthy fats like those in fish and nuts, and limited intake of alcohol and processed foods. Regular movement and good sleep can also help maintain liver health.
