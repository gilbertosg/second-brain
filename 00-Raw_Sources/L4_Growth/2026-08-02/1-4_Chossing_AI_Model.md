## Overview[cite: 1]

Claude Pro gives you access to several Claude models, each tuned for a different balance of speed, intelligence, and cost. Choosing the wrong one wastes time or produces weaker output than you need. This lesson gives you a decision framework, a task-based reference table, and four rules you will apply every time you open claude.ai.[cite: 1]

### Why This Matters[cite: 1]

Most people pick a model once and never change it. They either wait on the most powerful model for work that a faster one handles instantly, or they get subpar results by using the fastest one for complex reasoning. The right model for each task exists. This lesson shows you how to find it in under 10 seconds.[cite: 1]

## Step 1: Why Multiple Models Exist[cite: 1]

Think of AI models like hiring for different roles. You would not assign a senior director to answer routine emails. You would not ask an intern to develop your program strategy. Models follow the same logic. Each one is optimized for a different combination of speed, intelligence, and cost.[cite: 1]

### What You'll Do[cite: 1]

You will learn the three dimensions that separate models from each other and the one rule that governs every model selection decision.[cite: 1]

### Instructions[cite: 1]

1. Read the three dimensions in the table below.[cite: 1]
2. Notice that the dimensions trade off against each other. Faster models cost less but reason less deeply.[cite: 1]
3. Read the golden rule below the table. Commit it to memory.[cite: 1]
4. Keep this framework in mind as you review the three Claude models in Step 2.[cite: 1]

### Key Concepts[cite: 1]

| Dimension | What It Means | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Intelligence** | How well the model reasons, analyses, and handles complexity | Smarter models produce better outputs on hard tasks. They also cost more.[cite: 1] |
| **Speed** | How fast the model generates a response | Faster models save time on simple tasks. Speed and intelligence are often inversely related.[cite: 1] |
| **Cost** | How much each interaction costs, measured in tokens | Using an expensive model for simple tasks wastes budget with no quality gain.[cite: 1] |

## Step 2: The Model Family[cite: 1]

Claude comes in three models: Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus. They share the same strengths - writing, analysis, reasoning, and following complex multi-part instructions - and differ only in the balance of speed, intelligence, and cost. Learn these three and you can handle almost any task.[cite: 1]

### What You'll Do[cite: 1]

You will review the three Claude models and identify which one handles your most common task type.[cite: 1]

### Instructions[cite: 1]

1. Read the Claude table below. These three models cover the vast majority of daily work.[cite: 1]
2. Note Haiku for quick, simple tasks and Opus for your hardest reasoning.[cite: 1]
3. Skim the "Other Options" note so you know when a specialized tool fits.[cite: 1]
4. Identify one Claude model you are likely to use this week.[cite: 1]
5. Check your model picker in claude.ai regularly. Anthropic releases new models periodically.[cite: 1]

### The Three Claude Models[cite: 1]

Claude is your default for daily work. Each model trades speed against depth of reasoning.[cite: 1]

| Model | Best For | Key Specs |
|---|---|---|
| **Claude Haiku 4.5** | Quick questions, data formatting, simple classification, bulk repetitive tasks | Fastest. Lowest cost.[cite: 1] |
| **Claude Sonnet 4.5** | Reports, data analysis, meeting summaries, email drafting, most daily work | Balanced speed and quality. Your default.[cite: 1] |
| **Claude Opus 4.7** | Complex strategy, deep multi-step analysis, important deliverables, nuanced reasoning | Deepest reasoning. Slower. Premium.[cite: 1] |

### Other Options Worth Knowing About[cite: 1]

Claude does not process video or audio natively, and it does not generate images. For these niche tasks, a purpose-built tool from another provider is the right call. You do not need to learn these in depth - just know they exist.[cite: 1]

| Task | Tool to Use |
|---|---|
| Analyse a video or audio recording | Google Gemini (video and audio native)[cite: 1] |
| Generate an image from a text description | A dedicated image model (e.g., GPT Image 1 Mini)[cite: 1] |
| Transcribe audio to text only | A dedicated transcription model (e.g., Whisper)[cite: 1] |

### The Five-Question Decision Flow[cite: 2]

```text
1. Is the task simple and well-defined?
   (formatting, quick lookup, classification)
   -> Use Claude Haiku 4.5

2. Is the task standard knowledge work?
   (writing, summarizing, analyzing, drafting)
   -> Use Claude Sonnet 4.5 - YOUR DEFAULT

3. Does the task require deep reasoning?
   (strategy, complex decisions, high-stakes deliverables)
   -> Use Claude Opus 4.7

4. Does the task involve video or audio?
   -> Video and audio analysis: Google Gemini (Claude cannot do this)
   -> Transcription only: a dedicated transcription tool (e.g., Whisper)

5. Does the task involve generating images?
   -> Use a dedicated image tool (e.g., GPT Image 1 Mini)
```[cite: 2]

## Step 4: Speed, Cost, and Common Mistakes[cite: 2]

Every AI interaction uses tokens, and heavier models take longer and draw more from your plan's usage limits. Understanding the tradeoff helps you make better decisions - not just for your usage budget, but for response speed and output quality.[cite: 2]

### What You'll Do[cite: 2]

You will review the relative cost of each Claude model, learn what a token actually represents in terms of your documents, and internalize the four most common model selection mistakes.[cite: 2]

### Instructions[cite: 2]

1. Read the cost table. Focus on the Relative Cost column. Memorize two numbers: Sonnet costs roughly 3x Haiku. Opus costs roughly 5x Haiku.[cite: 2]
2. Read the token quick reference. Anchor the numbers to documents you work with regularly.[cite: 2]
3. Read the Do This / Avoid This columns. Identify which mistake you are most likely to make.[cite: 2]
4. Apply the golden rule from Step 1 to your next claude.ai session: use the lightest model that gets the job done.[cite: 2]

### Relative Cost Reference[cite: 2]

| Model | Relative Cost | Best Value For |
|---|---|---|
| **Claude Haiku 4.5** | **1x (baseline)** | **Simple, repetitive, high-volume tasks**[cite: 2] |
| **Claude Sonnet 4.5** | **~3x Haiku** | **Standard daily knowledge work (your default)**[cite: 2] |
| **Claude Opus 4.7** | **~5x Haiku** | **High-stakes reasoning only**[cite: 2] |

If your plan includes other providers, cheaper specialized options exist for niche multimedia tasks (video, audio, image generation). For everyday text work, the three Claude tiers above are all you need.[cite: 2]

### Token Quick Reference[cite: 2]

```text
1 token = ¾ of a word (about 4 characters)
1 page = 500 tokens
10 pages = 5,000 tokens
1-hour meeting transcript = 10,000-20,000 tokens
1M tokens = 750,000 words = 1,500 pages
```[cite: 2]

<!-- COLUMNS: 50/50 -->[cite: 2]

**Do This:**[cite: 2]

- Start with Sonnet for everything. Switch only when you have a specific reason.[cite: 2]
- Drop to Haiku for repetitive tasks. Formatting 50 similar items? Haiku is roughly 3x cheaper and just as accurate for pattern work.[cite: 2]
- Reach for Google Gemini only when you need video or audio. No Claude model processes them natively.[cite: 2]
- Check your model picker in claude.ai regularly. Anthropic releases new models periodically.[cite: 2]

<!-- COLSEP -->[cite: 2]

**Avoid This:**[cite: 3]

- Do not use Opus for simple questions. It is roughly 5x more expensive and slower. You will not notice a quality difference on easy tasks.[cite: 3]
- Do not push a very long document through Haiku. It has a smaller context window and may truncate your document. Use Sonnet for large documents.[cite: 3]
- Do not assume the heaviest model is always best. For simple tasks, lighter models produce identical results faster.[cite: 3]
- Do not run all work through one model out of habit. Matching the model to the task is a skill that pays off immediately.[cite: 3]
