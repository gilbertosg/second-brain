## Section 1: Career Growth

### 1.1 Managing Up — 7-Point 1:1 Structure

HBR data: managers retain only 60% of team contributions. McKinsey attribute this to information overload, not malice. The onus is on the individual contributor to close the gap.

**Weekly 1:1 agenda (use during Weekly Review prep):**

| # | Topic | Application for Gil |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Main Focus Areas | Confirm current week aligns to Principal promotion KRs |
| 2 | Progress Towards Deliverables | Show concrete movement on Agent Award, Serenity Scheduling |
| 3 | Plans for Next Week | Forward-looking: avoid being only reactive |
| 4 | Highlight Achievements | Frame achievements as org wins, not personal |
| 5 | Challenges & Roadblocks | Ask for active help — do not just report |
| 6 | Time Wasters | Quantify % of week lost to non-priority activity; propose reductions |

**Rule:** Never let "Time Wasters" go unaddressed. They are the data that prevents managers from overloading you.

### 1.2 PIE Model — The Career Advancement Multiplier

Source: Harvey Coleman, *Empowering Yourself: The Organizational Game Revealed* (1996)

| Factor | Weight | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Performance** | 10% | Delivering results |
| **Image** | 30% | How others perceive you — communication style, demeanor, brand |
| **Exposure** | 60% | Strategic visibility — who knows your contributions |

**Key insight:** High performance alone does not drive promotion. Exposure is 6x more powerful than performance in career advancement.

**The Sponsor distinction:** A sponsor actively advocates for you at the decision table. A mentor gives advice. Gil needs sponsors, not just mentors, for the Principal promotion.

**3-step PIE implementation:**
1. **Set goals** across all three dimensions (not just P)
2. **Self-assess** quarterly — ask peers for Image feedback (top 3 adjectives); count how many leaders know your work
3. **Close gaps** — track PIE progress in Quarterly Review milestones

**Gil application:** The Personal CRM (40 contacts) KR is an Exposure system. The 1:1 structure above is a Performance + Image system. Both must run in parallel.

### 1.3 Communication Norms — Team-Level System

Asynchronous communication consumes 60–77% of the workday (~185 interactions/day at ~2 min each = 6.2 hrs). Meetings consume 25–50% of the week.

**Two levers:**

**Meeting Fundamentals:**
- Set a written agenda before every meeting
- Invite the fewest people necessary
- Open recurring meetings with last session's action items
- End 5 min early: takeaways + owners + due dates
- Decline meetings without agendas (make this a team norm)

**Team Communication Norms (build once, run indefinitely):**
1. Survey team on current communication pain points
2. Define expected response times per channel
3. Establish no-meeting time blocks (not full days — time blocks to start)
4. Create a clear escalation protocol
5. Make norms a signed living agreement; track compliance
6. Add a 1–10 meeting satisfaction score to every meeting close

**TPM application:** At Blue Origin, building communication norms on any program team is a Principal-level leadership behavior. Running this exercise once earns Exposure and Image points simultaneously.

### 1.4 Decision-Making at Work — SPADE / DECIDE Frameworks

**Three biases to plan around:**
- **Groupthink:** Silence of lower-power voices; pre-brief stakeholders individually to get unbiased input before group sessions
- **Paradox of Choice:** 3 options is the optimal number for leadership decisions (more in appendix)
- **One-way vs. two-way doors:** Always ask how to make an irreversible decision reversible (experiment → full commit)

**How to make effective decisions:**
1. Define objective; narrow to ~3 options; add more to appendix
2. Pre-brief stakeholders individually (avoid groupthink in the room)
3. Make it a two-way door — identify experiments before committing
4. Set success criteria in advance (before you get attached to an option)
5. Use **SPADE**: Setting, People, Alternatives, Decide, Explain

**Gil's personal addition (from author):** When you end up creating a Frankenstein hybrid of options, build it proactively as a 4th option before the meeting starts. Add buffer for unknown-unknowns — plan for things nobody has done before.

### 1.5 Quitting When Ahead — "Is the Juice Worth the Squeeze?"

**The Dip (Seth Godin):** Temporary setbacks are worth pushing through. Dead ends are not. The skill is distinguishing between them.

**Biases that trap you:**
- Opportunity cost blindness — forgetting other options exist
- Sunk cost fallacy — past investment ≠ future obligation
- Emotional attachment — hardest to detect

**Practice:**
1. Regularly audit projects: is this a dip or a dead end?
2. Reassess milestones — are original goals still the right ones?
3. Analyze: do the benefits still justify the cost (time, energy, money)?
4. Seek an unbiased external perspective
5. Run small bets before making the full exit

**Gil application:** The E7 brand relaunch question — is each project cycle worth the Saturday bandwidth cost? Use this framework quarterly for the Passenger track.

### 1.6 Customer Obsession — Stakeholder Management Model

**Who are your "customers" at Blue Origin?**
- Your manager
- Your skip-level
- Peers on the promotion panel
- Direct reports (if any)
- Cross-functional partners on programs

**1,000 True Fans principle:** Don't try to please everyone. Identify the 10–15 stakeholders whose support is decisive for the Principal promotion, and serve them obsessively.

**Practice:**
1. Build a stakeholder map: name, last interaction, feedback received, follow-up plan
2. Know what keeps each key stakeholder up at night
3. Seek feedback continuously — not just at performance reviews
4. Show how you acted on feedback (closes the loop, builds trust)

**This is an Exposure system** that directly feeds the PIE model. Link to the Personal CRM KR.

### 1.7 Mentorship — How to Be a Great Mentee

**Don't ask "Will you be my mentor?"** — too formal, no clear value for the mentor. Instead: ask for 30 minutes on a specific problem. Keep it light and project-focused.

**What's in it for the mentor?** Make it visible. Send a follow-up showing you used their advice (even if you modified it). This closes the loop and earns a second meeting.

**Peer mentorship > senior mentorship** for tactical problems. Senior = sponsors. Peers = those who solved your exact problem 12 months ago.

**Mentor rolodex strategy:** Different people for different topics. No single mentor for everything. Bonus: ask your manager who their best direct report was — then get time with that person.

**Gil application:** Actively build a peer mentorship network at Blue Origin around the Principal competency areas (systems thinking, technical depth, cross-functional influence). These are also CRM contacts.

### 1.8 Psychological Safety — Foundation for Team Leadership

**4 levels (Amy Edmondson):**
1. Included — people feel they belong
2. Learning — safe to ask questions
3. Contributing — confident they can add value
4. Challenging — comfortable questioning the status quo

Google's Project Aristotle: psychological safety is the **single top predictor** of team success.

**Trust ≠ psychological safety:** Trust is individual confidence. Safety is the team-wide culture where everyone can be authentic without fear.

**Manager behaviors that build safety:**
- Share your own mistakes openly (model vulnerability)
- Create regular open-dialogue spaces
- Frame mistakes as learning moments, not blame events
- Use round-robin or direct invitations so quieter voices are heard
- Celebrate both big and small wins

**Gil application:** If managing a program team, psychological safety is the system that generates the "true fans" your promotion depends on. It also protects the team during high-stakes milestones.

### 1.9 Charisma (Rizz) — Social Capital as a Career Asset

Based on Bourdieu's Theory of Capital: professional influence depends more on networks and social connections than technical skills alone.

**EQ components that drive corporate charisma:**
- Active listening (engage, don't just hear)
- Situational adaptability (match the energy of the room)
- Body language alignment (maintain eye contact; open palms signal honesty)
- Appreciation systems (weekly thank-you reminder = relationship maintenance)

**Gil application:** Charisma is the behavioral mechanism of Exposure. It's how the PIE model's 60% actually gets deployed.

### 1.10 Email Etiquette — 7-Tip Async Communication System

1. **Subject lines that force priority decisions:** use `(Approval needed by EOD)` or `2min read |` brackets
2. **Minimize reply-all; note recipient changes explicitly**
3. **Structure:** Main point → context → offer to clarify → sign-off
4. **Acknowledge receipt with timeline** for complex emails
5. **Summarize when forwarding** (don't just "FYI")
6. **Use delayed send** to catch errors before delivery
7. **Slack/DM = urgent or brief only.** Anything > 2 min → email

## Section 2: Goal Setting (L3-Relevant)

### 2.1 Quarterly Review — The 1-in-60 Rule

One degree of error = 1 mile off course per 60 miles traveled. Quarterly Reviews exist to catch and correct drift before it compounds.

**12-minute quarterly retrospective:**
1. Open calendar + photos; create a 13-row table (one per week)
2. One-minute audit per week: what went high, what went low
3. Extract themes from highs (amplify) and lows (avoid/improve)
4. RAG (Red/Amber/Green) status each annual goal
5. For Amber or Red: Adjust expectations, remove goals, or review systems
6. Bonus: run with team/partner — shared accountability amplifies results

**Gil's existing system:** Gil uses OKRs and Weekly Reviews. This framework plugs directly into the Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4 milestone cadence in each pillar. The Quarterly Review is the meta-review on top of the weekly.

### 2.3 Goals as Systems — Input vs. Output Goals

Key distinction from James Clear + Sahil Bloom:
- **Output goals:** "Get promoted to Principal" (result-focused)
- **Input goals:** "Do 10 hrs Deep Work/week, 1 PIE conversation/month, 1 CRM update/week" (action-focused)

Output goals are uncontrollable. Input goals are the system.

**If behind at mid-year:** Use Sahil Bloom's ABC system — set Plans A, B, C so you always have measurable progress. Adapt, don't abandon.

**Parkinson's Law applied:** Work expands to fill the time available. Challenge: can you complete Principal-level work in 90% of your current time? This frees bandwidth for strategic (Exposure) activities.

### 2.4 The Harada Method — 64-Chart for Principal Promotion

Created by Takashi Harada; used by Shohei Ohtani.

**Structure:**
- 1 North Star goal at center (e.g., "Become Principal TPM")
- 8 supporting areas (e.g., technical depth, stakeholder visibility, team development, AI skills, communication, influence, domain knowledge, personal brand)
- 8 actions per area = 64 total supporting actions

This method enforces **daily routine alignment** with the long-term goal, not just quarterly milestone tracking.

**Gil caution (per author):** Remain flexible; use the 64-chart as a compass, not a cage. Pair with quarterly RAG reviews to allow revision.
