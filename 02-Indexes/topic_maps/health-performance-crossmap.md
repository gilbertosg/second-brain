**May 2026 key insight:** Total T improved to 300 ng/dL — the first positive signal since Aug 2023. But Free T at 54.0 pg/mL reveals this improvement is largely symbolic: SHBG and binding proteins are sequestering the biologically active fraction. The chain bottleneck is not testosterone production — it is **bioavailability**. This distinction determines whether the intervention pathway is lifestyle-only (SHBG reduction) or pharmacological (Clomiphene/TRT).

### Evidence

Low testosterone is clinically associated with:
- Fatigue (directly reduces available work hours)
- Difficulty focusing (degrades Deep Work quality — the primary career KR)
- Depression and low motivation (undermines initiative required for "Get path to Principal" KR)
- Loss of muscle mass (reduces energy and metabolic rate)
- Increased body fat (circular: fat tissue converts testosterone to estrogen, further suppressing T)

**Gil's testosterone trajectory:**
- Aug 2023: 399 ng/dL (peak — marathon training, ~85 kg body weight)
- Jan 2026: 262 ng/dL (−34% decline in 2.5 years)

At age 33, this decline is not biologically expected (natural testosterone decline begins ~35 at ~1-2%/year). The acceleration suggests lifestyle factors are driving it: weight gain (+8 kg from 2023 best), possible DHEA supplementation discontinuation (DHEA-S dropped 53%), and potentially elevated work stress.

## Intervention Priority Stack

Ordered by expected ROI on L3 career performance per unit of effort:

### Priority 1 — Free Testosterone Restoration (L1 → L3)

**Highest leverage action in the vault.** The bottleneck is Free T bioavailability, not total production. Restoring Free T from 54.0 pg/mL to the Medicine 3.0 optimal minimum (148.8 pg/mL) requires addressing SHBG binding, not just testosterone production. This would directly improve:
- Energy levels for Deep Work
- Cognitive focus and working memory
- Mood stability and motivation
- Muscle recovery from the lifting protocol

**Actions:**
1. **Physician consult (urgent) — Full hormone panel with Free T fractionation.** Request SHBG, Free T, LH, FSH, Estradiol alongside Total T. Free T at 54.0 pg/mL with Total T at 300 ng/dL suggests elevated SHBG binding. Evaluate Clomiphene citrate, Enclomiphene (preserves fertility), or direct TRT.
2. **Compound lower-body lifting** — Squats, deadlifts, hip hinges. The 2022 physician specifically noted: "lower body strength training helps maintain testosterone levels much more than smaller muscles." The Alpha/Beta/Gamma protocol (Barbell Back Squat + RDL + Bulgarian Split Squat) is optimally designed for this.
3. **Body fat reduction** — Fat tissue aromatizes testosterone to estrogen and may elevate SHBG. Every kg lost improves Free T availability. The -8 kg OKR directly supports this.
4. **Sleep 7:20 hr/night + Magnesium L-Threonate** — Testosterone pulses occur during deep sleep. L-Threonate (not Glycinate) crosses the blood-brain barrier to enhance slow-wave and REM sleep architecture.
5. **Alcohol ≤ 3x/week** — Alcohol impairs testosterone synthesis and elevates SHBG. The existing OKR is correct; execution is the variable.
6. **Caloric deficit ≤ 500 kcal/day** — Aggressive deficits suppress the HPG axis. Weight loss must be gradual to protect Free T production.

### Priority 2 — Homocysteine + MCH (L1 → L3, L4)

**Oct 2026 update:** MCH normalized at the last two draws (32.9, 32.6 pg; limit 33.0), which weakens the B12/folate inference below. Homocysteine read 10.0 umol/L in May (Function optimal 6–8; Quest limit 13.5) and was not retested. Keep homocysteine on the next draw.

Elevated homocysteine (7.55 Jan; 10.0 May) and persistently elevated MCH across 3 blood draws through Jan 2024 suggest a chronic B12/folate deficiency pattern. These nutrients are essential for:
- DNA synthesis and red blood cell production (MCH)
- Methylation cycle function (homocysteine)
- **Cognitive function and focus** (B12 deficiency = brain fog, memory issues)

**Actions:**
1. **Methylated B-complex daily** (as previously prescribed by Lifeforce in Mar 2023 — status unknown)
2. **Increase dietary B12 and folate** — asparagus, avocado, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, dark leafy vegetables
3. **Request MCH on next blood draw** — if still elevated after B-complex, escalate to physician for B12 serum level and reticulocyte testing

### Priority 3 — Liver and Metabolic Follow-Up (L1 → L1, L3 capacity)

The Oct panel added a second front. ALT 94 / AST 50 U/L, LDL particle number, triglycerides, GGT, and fasting insulin all moved up between May 19 and Oct 2 while glucose, HDL Large, and LDL Peak Size improved. The liver flags do not have an identified cause; medications, supplements, alcohol, recent training, and CK are unknown or unmeasured. The physician visit already pending for Free T can cover both. Detail and questions: [[L1-Health/liver-enzymes-alt-ast-oct2026]].

**Cross-pillar couplings to test, not assume:**
- **Alcohol OKR (≤ 3/week) touches three chains at once.** It appears in the Free T lever list above, the hs-CRP lever list, and now the liver differential. The vault stores the target and not the habit data, so no chain can be tested until the log exists.
- **Heavy-lift OKR (3/week) touches the liver and testosterone chains.** Lower-body lifting is the testosterone lever; intense or unaccustomed training is a recognized transient AST/ALT confounder (Function's AST and ALT pages). Training in the 72 hours before a draw belongs in the draw log.
- **Supplement stack touches the liver and hormone chains.** The creatine, Mg L-Threonate, omega-3, and D3/K2 stack and the Tier 1 DHEA, B-complex, and zinc plan need confirmation of what Gil takes now before a physician reads either panel.

### Priority 4 — Body Composition Normalization (L1 → L1 loop)

The 8 kg weight loss OKR creates a virtuous cycle:
- Weight loss → reduced fat mass → reduced testosterone-to-estrogen conversion → higher testosterone
- Higher testosterone → better muscle recovery → better body composition
- Lower body weight (historically at 85 kg) was associated with peak testosterone (399 ng/dL)

This is not a linear intervention — it is a self-reinforcing loop. The heaviest lifting work and sleep compliance will determine the speed of the cycle.
